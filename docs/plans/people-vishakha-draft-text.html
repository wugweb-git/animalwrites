Dr. Vishakha Shukla is the founder of Nawabi Tails Rescue Foundation in Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh. By the organisation's own count it has rescued more than 7,000 animals, and NDTV India reported in August 2025 that she looks after 400+ dogs at a farmhouse outside the city. The organisation says she trained as a dental surgeon and gave up her practice to do this work full time.

How it started

The organisation's own account says it began in 2020, during the COVID-19 lockdown, when Dr. Shukla and her team started rescuing animals from a three-bedroom apartment in Lucknow. The name goes back further, to her first year of medical school, when she rescued two puppies, Bella and Dude. The same account says she used her earnings to fund rescues until she left her practice to run Nawabi Tails full time. The site lists Vaibhav Shukla as co-founder.

How she works

Knocksense, which profiled the team in December 2023, reported that rescued dogs are neutered and sterilised and go back to the street only once they are ready. Animals with a disability, a terminal illness, old age or an abused past stay for good, and the rest are offered for adoption. She told Knocksense that adopting one animal saves three lives: that animal, the one who takes the freed space, and the mother who would otherwise keep having litters. The numbers, with their sources

The figures come from different places and dates, so they are not one count. The organisation's site lists 7,000+ rescues, 4,500+ rehabilitations and adoptions, and 350+ animals in its care now, with room for 450. Knocksense gave 1,500 animals rescued in 2021 and 1,306 in 2022. NDTV India reported 400+ dogs in her care in August 2025.

Her view on the law

After the Supreme Court's August 2025 order on street dogs, NDTV India reported her two doubts: what happens to feeding until feeding spots are set, and who decides what counts as aggressive. In January 2026 she wrote in the Hindustan Times that women who feed community dogs face harassment and asked the courts to act on their complaints. Working with others

Humane World for Animals India, which runs a large sterilisation and vaccination programme with the Lucknow Municipal Corporation, calls her organisation its long-term partner in the city. She is quoted there saying the programme's volunteer work has taught children, students and whole neighbourhoods to care for strays instead of fearing them.
