# Rabies facts for an Indian public health page: independent second check (18 Nov 2026)

Main primary source: **National Guidelines for Rabies Prophylaxis, 2019**, National Rabies Control Programme, NCDC/MoHFW (52 pages; the DGHS foreword is dated 24/02/2020; the PDF metadata title is "National Guidelines for Rabies Prophylaxis 2019"). It is hosted at https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf. The older link, ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf, now returns 404 after the NCDC site was rebuilt. The PDF is image-only with no text layer; quotes below were read from the rendered pages, and page numbers are the guideline's own printed numbers. Supporting source: NCDC expert group meeting minutes, 8 Jan 2019 — https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf. Note: the search tool claimed that "2015/2013" editions are the ones in circulation; that is wrong, because the 2019 edition is the one NCDC currently hosts.

## 1. "Wash the wound with soap under running water for about 15 minutes."

### Takeaway
**Correct.** Both NCDC 2019 and WHO say 15 minutes. NCDC says "running water". WHO says "at least 15 minutes", and NCDC's text says "desirable up to 15 minutes". "About 15 minutes" is fine. "At least 15 minutes" matches both the WHO wording and the NCDC tables.

### Cited Findings
- NCDC 2019, Table 1 (p.5), Cat I/II: "Wash exposed areas with soap and running water for 15 minute and then apply antiseptic" — [NCDC 2019](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf)
- NCDC 2019, Table 2 (p.6): "Wash all wounds under running tap water with soap for 15 minutes." §2.6.1.1: "Washing of wounds is desirable up to 15 minutes and should be carried out as soon as possible… wound management must be performed even if the patient reports late." — [NCDC 2019](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf)
- NCDC 2019, p.7 Don'ts: "Don't touch the wound(s) with the bare hands"; "Don't apply irritants like soil, chilies, oil, lime, herbs, chalk, betel leaves, etc." — same source
- 2019 expert meeting minutes, recommendation 1: "Washing of wound(s) is/are desirable up to 15 minutes and should be carried out as soon as possible with soap and water." — [NCDC minutes 8 Jan 2019](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)
- WHO fact sheet (page dated 17 Sep 2026): "wash the wound with water and soap for at least 15 minutes" — [WHO Rabies fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)

### Inferences
- A useful addition from NCDC: wash even if the person comes late, and apply an antiseptic (povidone iodine or alcohol) after washing.

### Gaps
- None.

## 2. "Start the anti-rabies vaccine the same day; the course runs over about a month."

### Takeaway
**Correct for India.** India has **not** adopted WHO 2018's shorter regimens. NCDC 2019 kept the IM 5-dose Essen schedule (days 0, 3, 7, 14, 28) and the ID Updated Thai Red Cross schedule (2-2-2-0-2: days 0, 3, 7, **28**). **The day-28 dose is still part of India's ID regimen**, so "about a month" is accurate. A common error: pages copied from WHO, CDC or US sources say "4 doses over 2 weeks" or "1-week ID regimen". That is wrong for Indian government practice.

### Cited Findings
- NCDC 2019 §4.1.3 (p.15): "Regimen for post exposure prophylaxis – Updated Thai Red Cross Schedule (2-2-2-0-2) … 4 Doses – 4 Visits: 2 ID vaccine doses (2 sites x 0.1 ml) on Days 0, 3, 7 and 28 … Day 0 is the date of administration of the first dose." — [NCDC 2019](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf)
- NCDC 2019 §4.2.1 (p.17): "Essen regimen (1-1-1-1-1): Five dose intramuscular schedule – the course … consists of … five injections, one dose each given on days 0, 3, 7, 14 and 28." Vaccine goes in the deltoid, or the anterolateral thigh in small children; "not in Gluteal Region". — same source
- NCDC 2019 ch.10 summary table (p.24): PEP ID 0.1 ml, Day 0, 3, 7 and 28, 2 injections per visit; PEP IM 1 entire vial, Day 0, 3, 7, 14 and 28. — same source
- NCDC 2019 ch.8 (p.22): "The PEP should be started as soon as patients report to the health facility, irrespective of time-lapse after the animal exposure … doses on day 0, day 3 and day 7 should be completed maximum within 10 days … One or two days deviation do not necessitate re-starting." — same source
- The 2019 expert group explicitly kept the older regimens instead of adopting WHO 2018: "Current Guidelines on post exposure prophylaxis … intradermal updated Thai Red Cross Schedule (2-2-2-0-2) and Intramuscular Regimen i.e. 4 weeks Essen regimen 1 Vial/Dose (1-1-1-1-1) retained." The chair cited "lack of evidence with respect to WHO recommendation in Indian settings". — [NCDC minutes 8 Jan 2019](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)
- NCDC 2019 §2.2 (p.3): a 4-dose IM course applies only if the dog or cat is still healthy after 10 days. PEP "can be converted to pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) by skipping the vaccine dose on day 14 and administering it on day 28 while using IM regimen". On the ID route, the complete course "should be given irrespective of the status of the animal". — [NCDC 2019](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf)
- WHO position paper dated 19 April 2018 (WER No 16, 2018, 93, 201–220): "replaces the 2010 WHO position" — [WHO WER 9316](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/who-wer9316)

### Inferences
- Suggested wording: "Start the vaccine the same day, even if the bite was days ago. Government clinics usually give 4 visits (days 0, 3, 7, 28) into the skin, or 5 injections (days 0, 3, 7, 14, 28) into the arm muscle. Finish every dose."

### Gaps
- I could not open the full WHO 2018 position paper text in this pass, so the WHO regimens are not quoted here: 1-week 2-site ID on days 0, 3, 7; IM 4-dose Essen on days 0, 3, 7 and one dose between 14 and 28; Zagreb 2-1-1. These are known from the literature but unverified in this check.
- I found no NCDC revision after 2019 (2020–2026) that adopts the WHO 1-week ID regimen. Some states or private clinics may differ.

## 3. "Category III bites need rabies immunoglobulin (RIG) as well as vaccine." Category definitions

### Takeaway
**Correct.** India is stricter than WHO here. WHO allows RIG to be prioritised when supply is short. India's 2019 expert group rejected that and requires RIG for **all** Category III exposures. NCDC also gives RIG in Category **II** for immunocompromised people. NCDC treats all wild animal exposures and all bites in forest areas as Category III. Bats are a difference: WHO counts direct bat contact as Category III, but NCDC says bat exposure does not currently warrant PEP in India.

### Cited Findings
- NCDC 2019 Table 1 (p.5):
  - **Cat I**: touching or feeding animals, licks on intact skin, contact of intact skin with secretions/excretions of a rabid animal or human case. Management: "None (if reliable case history is available)"; wash exposed areas.
  - **Cat II**: nibbling of uncovered skin, minor scratches or abrasions without bleeding. Management: wound management plus rabies vaccine.
  - **Cat III**: single or multiple transdermal bites or scratches, licks on broken skin, contamination of mucous membrane with saliva from licks. Management: wound management, rabies immunoglobulin and rabies vaccine.
  - Note: "Bites by wild animals and all bites in forest areas should be considered as Category III." — [NCDC 2019](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf)
- NCDC 2019 §2.7 (p.8): "RIG should be administered to all patients with category III exposure." Box: "In immunocompromised individuals such as HIV/AIDS patients, patients on immunosuppressive therapy … RIG should be administered in both Category II and III exposure." — same source
- NCDC 2019 §2.9 (p.9): RIG is given once, as soon as possible after starting vaccination, and "not indicated beyond the seventh day after the first dose". Doses: HRIG 20 IU/kg, ERIG 40 IU/kg, infiltrated into and around the wounds. — same source
- 2019 minutes, rec. 7: "The group did not accept WHO recommendation on prioritization of Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG) in category III exposures. It is recommended that all the patients with Cat-III exposure RIG should be given." — [NCDC minutes 8 Jan 2019](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)
- WHO: Cat I "Washing of exposed skin surfaces, no PEP"; Cat II "Wound washing and immediate vaccination"; Cat III (including "direct contact with bats") "Wound washing, immediate vaccination and administration of rabies immunoglobulin/monoclonal antibodies" — [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)
- NCDC 2019 §2.1 (p.3): "Exposure to bats: Bat rabies has not been conclusively proven in India and hence, at present, exposure to bats does not warrant PEP." — [NCDC 2019](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf)
- People with documented complete earlier PrEP or PEP get 2 doses (day 0 and day 3) and **no RIG** (ch.5, p.19). — same source

### Inferences
- Plain wording: "If the bite or scratch bleeds or breaks the skin, or saliva got on broken skin, eyes or mouth, you need the injection into the wound (RIG) as well as the vaccine."
- WHO and DCGI also allow monoclonal antibodies in place of RIG. NCDC 2019 (ch.9) says their role "needs to be studied" before it is added to the national guidelines.

### Gaps
- None material.

## 4. "Rabies is almost always fatal once symptoms appear; there is no reliable cure."

### Takeaway
**Correct.** WHO says "virtually 100% fatal". NCDC says "nearly 100% fatal" and "almost 100% case fatality rate". Avoid "always fatal" or "100% fatal", because a handful of survivors are documented worldwide.

### Cited Findings
- WHO: "Once clinical symptoms appear, rabies is virtually 100% fatal." — [WHO fact sheet, dated 17 Sep 2026](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)
- NCDC 2019 Preface: "Rabies is one of the oldest recognized Zoonotic disease with almost 100% case fatality rate." §2.5 (p.4): "As rabies is a nearly 100% fatal disease, there is no contraindication to PEP." — [NCDC 2019](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf)

### Inferences
- The NCDC point that pregnancy and infancy are not reasons to skip PEP is useful page copy (§2.5 and FAQ 10).

### Gaps
- I did not search for current survivor counts. They are not needed for the claim as worded.

## 5. "Symptoms usually appear 2–3 months after a bite, but can range from under a week to over a year."

### Takeaway
**Needs small rewording.** WHO says "typically 2–3 months but may vary from one week to one year". "Under a week" and "over a year" go slightly beyond WHO's wording. They are documented in case literature, but a public page should mirror WHO: "usually 2–3 months; it can be as short as a week or as long as a year or more".

### Cited Findings
- WHO: "The incubation period for rabies is typically 2–3 months but may vary from one week to one year, depending on factors such as the location of virus entry and the viral load." — [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)
- NCDC 2019 §2.5 (p.4): "people who present for treatment even months or years after a possible rabies exposure should be evaluated and treated as if the event had occurred recently." — [NCDC 2019](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf)

### Inferences
- The practical message from NCDC is that a late visit is still worth it.

### Gaps
- I did not pull a primary source for incubation periods over 1 year in this pass.

## 6. Rat and small-rodent bites

### Takeaway
**Correct in substance, but needs India-specific wording.** NCDC does not say rodent bites are "very low risk". It says domestic rodents, hares and rabbits do not *ordinarily* require PEP, **but rodent bites in forest areas need PEP**. The guideline also says to offer PEP when the animal's identity is unreliable (mole or mongoose mistaken for a rat). Wounds should still be washed, and tetanus cover may be needed. WHO: "bites from rodents are not known to transmit rabies."

### Cited Findings
- NCDC 2019 §2.1 (p.3): "Rodent Bites: Exposure to domestic rodents, hare and rabbits do not ordinarily require PEP. However, rodent bites in forest areas necessitate institution of PEP." — [NCDC 2019](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf)
- NCDC 2019 FAQ 13 (p.35): "Under normal circumstances, rabies is not transmitted by domestic rats. As a general rule, it is therefore not necessary to receive PEP if bitten by a rat but the wound should be cleaned and washed as in case of any other animal bite wound. Consult physician for other care, e.g. anti-tetanus prophylaxis, if required. However, in many cases, the history given by the patient seems unreliable due to similarly looking species such as mole, mongoose etc, in such cases, to be on safer side the PEP should always be offered to the victim." — same source
- WHO: "bites from rodents are not known to transmit rabies." — [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)

### Inferences
- Suggested wording: "Bites from house rats and mice usually do not need the rabies vaccine in India. Still wash the wound and see a doctor. Get the vaccine if the bite happened in a forest, or if you are not sure what animal it was (for example, a mongoose)."
- Note that mongooses are rabies vectors in India: NCDC Intro says dogs cause about 95%, cats 2%, and "jackals, mongoose and others (1%)".

### Gaps
- None.

## 7. "Most dogs do not have rabies; a vaccinated dog is very unlikely to have it. After a bite that breaks the skin, start the vaccine anyway."

### Takeaway
**Second sentence correct; first sentence needs rewording.** NCDC is explicit: do **not** wait for a 10-day observation, and the dog's vaccination status does **not** change the decision, because in India "every animal bite is suspected as a potentially rabid animal bite". "A vaccinated dog is very unlikely to have it" is true in principle, but NCDC warns that vaccination cannot be presumed effective without proof. The page should not lead with reassurance that could delay PEP. Also, the trigger is not only a bite that breaks the skin: minor scratches or abrasions without bleeding (Cat II) also need the vaccine.

### Cited Findings
- NCDC 2019 §2.1 (p.3): "In a rabies endemic country like India … every animal bite is suspected as a potentially rabid animal bite, and treatment should be started immediately after exposure." — [NCDC 2019](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf)
- NCDC 2019 §2.2 (p.3): "The PEP should be started immediately after the exposure. The observation period of 10 days is valid for dogs and cats only." — same source
- NCDC 2019 §2.3 (p.3–4): "Animals vaccinated against rabies do not suffer and transmit the disease. However, animal vaccine failures may occur because of improper administration, inadequate doses, poor quality of the vaccine or poor health status of the animal. Thus, the history of Rabies vaccination in an animal does not guarantee that the biting animal is not rabid. … Hence, irrespective of the vaccination status of the biting animal, the PEP should be given." — same source
- NCDC 2019 §2.4: "A bite by a provoked animal does not mean that the animal is not rabid." — same source
- NCDC 2019 FAQ 9 (p.34): "Is simply observing the biting animal for 10 days without starting PEP justified? Answer: No. … it is necessary to start post-exposure prophylaxis for the exposed person promptly and keep the biting animal under observation for 10 days where possible." — same source

### Inferences
- Suggested wording: "Even if the dog looks healthy or is vaccinated, start the vaccine for any bite or scratch that breaks or grazes the skin. Do not wait to watch the dog for 10 days. If a dog or cat is still healthy after 10 days, your doctor may shorten the course."

### Gaps
- I found no reliable figure for "most dogs do not have rabies" in India in this pass. If kept, it should be unquantified.

## 8. "Anti-rabies vaccine is free at government hospitals in India under the National Rabies Control Programme."

### Takeaway
**Needs rewording on the mechanism.** ARV **and** RIG (ARS) are free at public health facilities, but under the **National Free Drug Initiative (NFDI) of the National Health Mission**, procured by states. They are not funded through the NRCP: an older PIB reply says NRCP funds go only to training, surveillance, labs and advocacy. RIG is also free, at least officially. Actual availability varies by facility and state.

### Cited Findings
- Rajya Sabha written reply, MoS Health Prataprao Jadhav, 19 Aug 2025 (PIB PRID 2157880): "Anti-Rabies Vaccine (ARV) and Anti-Rabies Serum (ARS)/Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG) are provided free of cost in the public health facilities under National Free Drug Initiative of NHM." — [PIB release 2157880](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2157880&reg=48&lang=2). The quote comes from the search-result summary; the direct fetch returned HTTP 403, so verify the exact text on PIB.
- Health Minister J.P. Nadda in the Lok Sabha: no reports of a nationwide ARV shortage; states are advised to keep up ARV/RIG supply through NFDI funds; health is a state subject. — [Outlook India report](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/no-reports-on-nationwide-shortage-of-anti-rabies-vaccine-nadda-tells-lok-sabha-2). This is secondary; the sansad.in primary PDF (AU275) timed out.
- Earlier PIB Lok Sabha reply (MoS Ashwini Kumar Choubey; exact date not confirmed): NRCP funds "are only provided for training, surveillance, laboratory strengthening and advocacy". States were asked to include ARV/ARS in essential drug lists and procure them under NFDI/NHM. — [PIB erelcontent 274889](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/erelcontent.aspx?relid=274889&reg=3&lang=2) (search summary only)
- NCDC 2019 Annexure VI, item 8: "Anti-rabies vaccine and Anti rabies serum is part of an essential drug list of National Health Mission (NHM). Budget for Human Rabies Vaccine & Anti Rabies Serum may be proposed under NHM PIP under National Free Drug Initiative." — [NCDC 2019](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf)
- ICMR study in Lancet Regional Health – Southeast Asia (reported): about 80% of public health facilities had ARV available, so availability is not universal. — [Medical Dialogues report](https://health.medicaldialogues.in/health/study-finds-major-gaps-across-indias-rabies-vaccine-availability-151548) (secondary; not opened)

### Inferences
- Suggested wording: "The rabies vaccine and rabies immunoglobulin are free at government hospitals and health centres in India. If one centre has run out, ask to be referred to the nearest anti-rabies clinic."
- I did not verify whether ARV and RIG are on the National List of Essential Medicines (NLEM 2022). NLEM is a pricing/essential list, separate from NFDI free supply. Do not claim NLEM status without checking.

## 9. India rabies burden figures

### Takeaway
**The "~18,000–20,000 deaths a year" figure is outdated and should not be used as current.** It traces to a 2003/2004 APCRI survey (about 20,565 deaths a year) and is repeated in the 2019 guideline foreword and in many news stories. The current peer-reviewed estimate is **5,726 deaths a year (95% UI 3,967–7,350)**. It comes from the ICMR-NIE community survey (2022–23), published in *The Lancet Infectious Diseases* (online Sept 2024; print Jan 2025, vol 25 issue 1; DOI 10.1016/S1473-3099(24)00490-0). The same study estimates **9.1 million animal bites a year** (6.6 per 1,000 people). Official IDSP/NRCP hospital-reported deaths are far lower, in the low hundreds; NCDC 2019 says "130–210" a year. That reflects under-reporting, not the true burden.

### Cited Findings
- ICMR-NIE study: "5726 (95% uncertainty interval 3967–7350) human rabies deaths occurring annually in India"; bites 6.6 (95% CI 5.7–7.6) per 1000, about 9.1 million nationally; survey March 2022–Aug 2023, 60 districts, 15 states, 78,800+ households; 20.5% of dog-bite victims got no ARV. — [Amrita University publication record](https://www.amrita.edu/publication/estimates-of-the-burden-of-human-rabies-deaths-and-animal-bites-in-india-2022-23-a-community-based-cross-sectional-survey-and-probability-decision-tree-modelling-study/); [DTNext/PTI report](https://www.dtnext.in/lifestyle/wellbeing/lancet-study-estimates-three-in-every-four-animal-bites-in-india-due-to-dogs-820523). This is from the abstract via secondary sources; the full text was not opened.
- NCDC 2019 foreword (DGHS, dated 24/02/2020): "some studies estimate that 174 lakh persons are bitten and approximately 20,000 persons succumb to the disease annually." Programme officer's note: "About 130–210 rabies deaths in hospital are reported every year under rabies control programme and approximately 6–7 Million animal bites are reported annually under IDSP." — [NCDC 2019](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf)
- 2019 minutes cite "APCRI-WHO projected research data in 2004 of 17.4 million animal bite in India". — [NCDC minutes](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)
- WHO global figure (Global Health Estimates 2021 model): "rabies causes 44 203 annual deaths", likely an underestimate. The current fact sheet gives **no India-specific share**. — [WHO fact sheet, 17 Sep 2026](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)

### Inferences
- The often-quoted "India accounts for 36% of world rabies deaths" and "18,000–20,000 deaths" both come from older WHO/APCRI-era estimates. Neither appears in the current WHO fact sheet as checked. Flag them as out of date.
- Suggested wording: "An ICMR study published in 2024 estimated about 5,700 rabies deaths and 9 million animal bites in India each year."

### Gaps
- I did not open the full Lancet ID paper.
- I did not retrieve the latest official IDSP/NRCP reported death counts for 2024–2026 (Lok Sabha answers would have them).

## 10. Abhayrab and counterfeit batches

### Takeaway
**Needs rewording.** Abhayrab is made by **Human Biologicals Institute (HBI), a subsidiary/division of Indian Immunologicals Ltd (IIL)**, Hyderabad. IIL describes it as a "lyophilized, purified, inactivated Rabies vaccine" made from the L. Pasteur 2061/Vero strain grown in Vero cells. "Purified Vero cell rabies vaccine (PVRV)" is an accurate category, though IIL's page does not use that exact phrase. **Counterfeit alert confirmed:** Australia's ATAGI issued a statement in Dec 2025 (updated Jan 2026) and Victoria Health issued an alert on 23 Dec 2025. Both say counterfeit Abhayrab batches have circulated in India **since 1 Nov 2023**, and anyone who got Abhayrab in India from that date should be offered replacement doses. IIL disputes the scope and says it was one batch (KA24014), removed from supply. **A newer incident:** in Sept 2026, Abhayrab vials (batch KE25012) seized by CDSCO from unlicensed premises in Delhi were reported "Not of Standard Quality and misbranded". IIL says those vials were not made by HBI.

### Cited Findings
- IIL product page: "lyophilized, purified, inactivated Rabies vaccine"; "L. Pasteur 2061/Vero Strain propagated in Vero cells"; "0.5 ml to be administered by deep intramuscular route". The page does not name HBI. — [Indian Immunologicals, AbhayRab PF](https://www.indimmune.com/?p=1977)
- HBI is "a subsidiary of India Immunologicals" — [Khaleej Times, 5 Sep 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/asia/india-immunologicals-statement-abhayrab-rabies-vaccine-found-substandard)
- Victoria Health alert, issued and updated 23 Dec 2025: "Counterfeit batches of rabies vaccine Abhayrab® have been circulating in India since November 2023." People who "received rabies vaccine in India from 1 November 2023 onwards" should see their GP, and doses should be replaced with Rabipur® or Verorab®. — [Health Victoria](https://health.vic.gov.au/health-alerts/counterfeit-rabies-vaccine-abhayrab-in-india)
- ATAGI statement "Counterfeit Rabies vaccine (Abhayrab®) reported in India: Guidance for travellers and healthcare providers": Dec 2025 PDF, revised Jan 2026 (file "_0", reportedly v1.1). Per search summaries: Abhayrab given in India from 1 Nov 2023, or a vaccine of unknown brand, should be treated as potentially invalid; HRIG does not need replacing; genuine packs have a pink flip-off seal and counterfeits a blue one. — [ATAGI Jan 2026 PDF](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-01/atagi-statement-counterfeit-rabies-vaccine-abhayrab-reported-in-india-guidance-for-travellers-and-healthcare-providers_0.pdf); [ATAGI Dec 2025 PDF](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/atagi-statement-counterfeit-rabies-vaccine-abhayrab-reported-in-india-guidance-for-travellers-and-healthcare-providers.pdf). The PDF download failed with an HTTP/2 error, so the details here come from search summaries.
- IIL response: the issue was an isolated batch, KA24014 (Mfg Mar 2024, Exp Feb 2027), identified and removed; IIL asked ATAGI to review the advisory. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/australia-warns-against-counterfeit-rabies-vaccine-supplied-in-india-maker-says-advisory-misplaced/2813456); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/health/fake-rabies-vaccine-row-iil-says-counterfeit-batch-no-longer-available-125122700424_1.html); [Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/hyderabad-based-iil-seeks-review-of-australian-advisory-on-abhayrab-vaccine)
- Sept 2026: CDSCO drew vials from unlicensed premises in Delhi and reported them "Not of Standard Quality (NSQ) and misbranded" (batch KE25012). IIL says they "are not a product manufactured by Human Biologicals Institute". — [Khaleej Times, 5 Sep 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/asia/india-immunologicals-statement-abhayrab-rabies-vaccine-found-substandard)
- Side note: Annexure I of NCDC 2019 (DCGI-approved ARV makers) lists Human Biological Limited (sic) Hyderabad, Cadila, Chiron Behring, Serum Institute and Bharat Biotech. "Human Biological Limited, Hyderabad" appears to be HBI/IIL. — [NCDC 2019](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf)

### Inferences
- Suggested wording: "Abhayrab is a rabies vaccine grown in Vero cells, made by Human Biologicals Institute, part of Indian Immunologicals Ltd." For a public page, do not imply that Abhayrab is unsafe in general. If mentioned, say that foreign health authorities flagged fake batches sold in India since Nov 2023, that the maker says the problem was limited, and that people should get vaccinated at a government or trusted clinic and keep the vial and batch details.
- Two separate incidents (Dec 2025 counterfeit; Sept 2026 NSQ/misbranded) should not be merged into one.
