# Feeding community (stray) dogs in India: legal position as of 18 Nov 2026

Researched 18 Nov 2026. Primary texts read in full: the Supreme Court orders of 22 Aug 2025 and 7 Nov 2025, and the judgment of 19 May 2026 (PDFs from api.sci.gov.in, converted to text), plus the Bombay HC order of 2 Aug 2024 that quotes ABC Rules 2023 rule 20 word for word. Everything else comes from news or legal-news reports, and each item says so.

**Most important finding for the explainer:** the Supreme Court's 19 May 2026 judgment (2026 INSC 506) is the current controlling text. It keeps the 22 Aug 2025 street-feeding ban and the 7 Nov 2025 institutional-removal directions in force nationwide, rejects the challenges to them, and hands monitoring to the High Courts. The next Supreme Court date was 17 Nov 2026 (yesterday). I found no report of what happened at that hearing.

## Q1. ABC Rules 2023, rule 20: feeding, RWA duties, feed spots, Animal Welfare Committee

### Takeaway
Rule 20 of the Animal Birth Control Rules, 2023 (in force from 10 March 2023) makes the RWA, Apartment Owner Association (AOA) or local body representative responsible for arranging feeding of community animals. They must do this with the residents who feed, at mutually agreed feed spots and times. Disputes go to an Animal Welfare Committee, whose decision on the feeding point is final, with an appeal to the State Board. Read with the 2025 and 2026 Supreme Court orders, the rule still governs feeding inside residential premises and areas. It no longer permits street feeding outside designated spots (see Q4), and it does not cover institutional premises (see Q4).

### Cited Findings
- The rules were notified as G.S.R. 193(E) on 10 March 2023 under the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960, and replaced the Animal Birth Control (Dog) Rules, 2001 — [Insights on India summary](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2023/04/21/animal-birth-control-rules-2023/); the Bombay HC also notes the rules "entered force on 10th March 2023" — [RNA Royal Park CHS v MCGM, Bombay HC, WP 3626/2024, 2 Aug 2024 (PDF)](https://verdictum.in/pdf_upload/rna-royal-park-co-operative-housing-society-v-municipal-corporation-of-greater-mumbaiwatermark-1643078.pdf)
- **Exact text of rule 20, as quoted by the Bombay HC (Sonak and Khata JJ.) on 2 Aug 2024** — [RNA Royal Park order (PDF)](https://verdictum.in/pdf_upload/rna-royal-park-co-operative-housing-society-v-municipal-corporation-of-greater-mumbaiwatermark-1643078.pdf):
  - 20(1): "It shall be responsibility of the Resident Welfare Association or Apartment Owner Association or Local Body's representative of that area to make necessary arrangement for feeding of community animals residing in the premises or that area involving the person residing in that area or premises as the case may be, who feeds those animals or intends to feed those animals and provides care to street animals as a compassionate gesture." The RWA, AOA or local body representative "shall ensure":
    - (i) "to designate feed spots which are mutually agreed upon, keeping in mind the number of dog population and their respective territories and the feeding spots shall be far from children play areas, entry and exit points, staircase or in an area which is likely to be least frequented by children and senior citizen."
    - (ii) "to designate feeding time depending on the movement of children, senior citizens, sports which is likely to be least frequented by children and senior citizen."
    - (iii) "designated feeder shall ensure that there is no littering at the feeding location or violation of guidelines framed by the Resident Welfare Association or Apartment Owner Association or that areas."
    - (iv) "designated feeders are allowed to volunteer for the vaccination, catching and release of dogs to assist with the Animal Birth Control Program."
  - 20(2): "Where there is any conflict between the Resident Welfare Association or Apartment Owner Association and the animal caregivers or other residents, an Animal Welfare committee comprising of the following members shall be formed". The members are: (i) the Chief Veterinary Officer or a representative; (ii) a representative of the jurisdictional police; (iii) a representative of the District SPCA or State Board; (iv) a representative of a recognised AWO doing ABC; (v) a Veterinary Officer deputed by the local authority; (vi) the complainant; (vii) a representative of the RWA, AOA or local body. "The decision of the Committee ... shall be the final decision with regard to the fixing of the feeding point and the Committee may also nominate person from amongst the designated Colony Care Taker by the Board to feed those animals in that area."
  - 20(3): anyone aggrieved (the local authority, an AWO, a feeder, the RWA, the AOA or the local body) appeals to the State Board, and "the decision of State Board shall be the final decision for feeding of animals in that area."
- In the same case, the Bombay HC sent an RWA-versus-feeder dispute to the rule 20(2) committee rather than deciding it itself. It directed the municipal corporation (MCGM, Mumbai) to form an Animal Welfare Committee within 15 days and decide the complaint within 15 days after that, hearing both sides and visiting the premises. The court said breaches of feeding-point protocols, breaches of timings and aggression of the animals are "matters that are best considered by the Animal Welfare Committee" — [RNA Royal Park order, paras 9–12](https://verdictum.in/pdf_upload/rna-royal-park-co-operative-housing-society-v-municipal-corporation-of-greater-mumbaiwatermark-1643078.pdf)
- Rule 7(2) classifies "street dogs" or "community owned dogs" as dogs that are homeless and found on streets or within a gated campus. Rule 11(19) requires release of sterilised dogs to the place they were picked up from. On 19 May 2026 the Supreme Court held rule 7(2) to be "merely a classificatory provision" that does not create "any substantive or vested right" for dogs to stay in a space. It held that "gated campus" must be read narrowly so that it does not cover hospitals, schools, colleges, sports complexes, airports and similar places — [SC judgment 19 May 2026, paras 42–44 (PDF)](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/41706/41706_2025_2_1501_71480_Judgement_19-May-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- Rule 20 contains no power for an RWA to ban feeding outright or to fine feeders. The RWA's duty is to arrange feeding with the feeders, and a dispute's binding outcome comes from the committee or the State Board, not from the RWA alone.
- The 19 May 2026 reading of "gated campus" is about institutions. It does not expressly address residential housing societies, so rule 20 feed spots inside housing societies appear unaffected. This is my inference: the judgment does not deal with housing societies directly.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the egazette.gov.in copy of G.S.R. 193(E). The wording above comes from the Bombay HC's quotation and should be checked against the Gazette before publishing exact quotes (note the odd phrases "that areas" and "sports which is likely", which may be original drafting).

## Q2. AWBI advisories and guidelines on feeding; stance on harassment of feeders

### Takeaway
Before the 2025 Supreme Court orders, AWBI issued advisories on feeding: in 2015 to state police chiefs on harassment of people who show compassion to animals, and on 3 March 2021 on designated feeding spots and no harassment of feeders at those spots. After the 7 Nov 2025 order, AWBI issued SOPs dated 27 Nov 2025 for institutional premises. These include "feeding protocols" and extend to parks, religious places and similar areas. The Supreme Court upheld the SOPs on 19 May 2026.

### Cited Findings
- The Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying (PIB) lists AWBI advisories that include a 2015 circular on pet and street dogs, 2015 circulars to state police chiefs on harassment of citizens who show compassion to animals, and a 2021 advisory on designating feeding spots — [PIB release PRID 1881462](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1881462) (as summarised in search results; I did not read the page in full)
- The AWBI letter of 3 March 2021, addressed to the Delhi Chief Secretary and Police Commissioner, is reported to direct enforcement agencies to ensure "no harassment or hindrance is caused to the person feeding street dog at the designated feeding spot" — reported via search summary; the AWBI file is an image scan I could not OCR: [AWBI PDF, file created 4 Mar 2021](https://awbi.gov.in/uploads/regulations/163310193039feeding%20spots%20for%20stray%20dogs.pdf)
- AWBI SOPs dated 27 Nov 2025 were issued under Direction (J) of the 7 Nov 2025 order. They cover identification of shelters, shelter capacity, "feeding protocols", waste management, fencing, nodal officers, removal, sterilisation and relocation of dogs to shelters, community kennels, and "structured feeding protocols". They extend to "religious places, children's parks, airports, helipads, seaports, tourist sites and recreational areas" — [SC judgment 19 May 2026, paras 77–78](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/41706/41706_2025_2_1501_71480_Judgement_19-May-2026.pdf)
- The SC dismissed every challenge to the SOPs, holding that the list of institutional areas in the 7 Nov order was "not intended to be exhaustive". Parks, religious sites and tourist places "stand on a similar footing" — [SC judgment 19 May 2026, paras 82–85](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/41706/41706_2025_2_1501_71480_Judgement_19-May-2026.pdf)
- In November 2022, AWBI's counsel told the SC: "If these stray dogs are not fed, they will become more aggressive" — [Bar & Bench, Nov 2022](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/supreme-court-says-no-fine-can-imposed-feeding-stray-dogs-stays-bombay-high-court-observations)

### Inferences
- The 2021 advisory protects feeders at designated spots, which fits the SC's 22 Aug 2025 rule (feeding only at designated spots). Feeders outside designated spots should not rely on it.

### Gaps
- I did not read the text of the 27 Nov 2025 SOPs, so their exact feeding protocol (times, distances, who may feed) is unknown. Fetch it from awbi.gov.in.
- I found no AWBI advisory dated after Nov 2025 on harassment of feeders, and I could not confirm whether the 2015 and 2021 advisories have been withdrawn or revised.

## Q3. Delhi HC, Dr Maya D. Chablani v Radha Mittal (24 June 2021)

### Takeaway
On 24 June 2021, J.R. Midha J. of the Delhi HC (CS(OS) 277/2020) held that community dogs have a right to food and citizens have a right to feed them, at designated spots and without harassing others. The judgment put duties on RWAs and municipal bodies. **The Supreme Court stayed it on 4 March 2022**, and the 2025–26 SC orders now set the national rule. The explainer should not present Chablani as current law without that caveat.

### Cited Findings
- The judgment holds that stray or street dogs have the right to food and citizens are entitled to feed them, without causing harm or harassment to others. Every RWA or municipal corporation must ensure dogs in its area have food and water where there are no caregivers. Feeding must be at areas designated by AWBI in consultation with RWAs or municipal corporations. RWAs, municipal bodies and police must ensure no hindrance is caused to caregivers — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/stray-dogs-have-the-right-to-food-citizens-right-to-feed-stray-dogs-delhi-high-court); judgment text: [Delhi HC PDF, 2021:DHC:1906](https://delhihighcourt.nic.in/app/case_number_pdf/2021%3ADHC%3A1906/JRM24062021S2772020_194604.pdf)
- The SC (Vineet Saran and Aniruddha Bose JJ.) stayed the Delhi HC order on Friday 4 March 2022, on an appeal by the NGO Humane Foundation for People and Animals, and issued notice to AWBI and the Delhi government — [Scroll, 5 Mar 2022](https://scroll.in/latest/1018814/scroll_in)

### Inferences
- The SC suo motu orders transferred High Court matters on stray dogs to the SC, and the 19 May 2026 judgment sent them back to the High Courts. Whether the Chablani SLP is among them, and whether the stay still stands, is unclear.

### Gaps
- Current status of the SC appeal against Chablani: I could not confirm whether it was disposed of, tagged with the suo motu case, or is still pending under stay.
- A later Delhi HC judgment (CM(M) 431/2023, 2025) is reported to cite Chablani. I did not read it: [Delhi HC PDF](https://delhihighcourt.nic.in/app/showFileJudgment/60902042025CMM4312023_183404.pdf)

## Q4. Supreme Court suo motu case (2025–2026): orders and current status

Case: **In Re: "City Hounded by Strays, Kids Pay Price", Suo Motu Writ Petition (Civil) No. 5 of 2025**, with connected SLPs, transfer cases and a contempt petition.

### Takeaway
- 11 Aug 2025: a two-judge bench ordered Delhi-NCR dogs removed to shelters.
- 22 Aug 2025: a three-judge bench (Vikram Nath, Sandeep Mehta, N.V. Anjaria JJ.) put the no-release part in abeyance. Dogs are to be released after sterilisation and vaccination, except rabid and aggressive dogs. Street feeding is banned, with feeding only in dedicated spaces in each municipal ward, and action against violators. The case was expanded to all States and UTs.
- 7 Nov 2025: the same bench ordered dogs removed from institutional premises (schools, hospitals, sports complexes, bus stands, railway stations) to shelters, with no re-release there.
- 19 May 2026 (reported judgment, 2026 INSC 506): the bench dismissed every application to modify or recall those directions and upheld the AWBI SOPs. It allowed euthanasia of rabid, incurably ill or dangerous dogs. It required animal-welfare groups that feed on campuses to accept tortious liability by affidavit. It transferred monitoring to all High Courts, with the next SC listing on 17 Nov 2026.
- These are directions under Article 142 and continuing mandamus. The case is not closed: the 19 May ruling is a reported judgment on the applications, but the matter remains listed for further directions.

### Cited Findings
**22 Aug 2025 order (three-judge bench; reportable order, para 33 onwards)** — [SC order PDF, 22 Aug 2025](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/41706/41706_2025_3_1501_63567_Order_22-Aug-2025.pdf)
- Bench: Vikram Nath, Sandeep Mehta and N.V. Anjaria JJ. Para 33 says the directions "issued by the two-Judge bench of this Court are supplemented, modified, and clarified".
- 33(b): the bar on release is "kept in abeyance for the time being". Dogs that are picked up "shall be sterilised, dewormed, vaccinated, and released back to the same area from which they were picked up". This does not apply to dogs "infected with rabies or suspected to be infected with rabies, and those that display aggressive behaviour". Those dogs are to be sterilised and immunised but "under no circumstances" released back, and kept in a separate pound or shelter as far as possible.
- 33(d), verbatim: "Municipal authorities shall forthwith commence an exercise for creating dedicated feeding spaces for the stray dogs in each municipal ward. The feeding areas shall be created/identified keeping in view the population and concentration of stray dogs in the particular municipal ward. Gantries/notice boards shall be placed near such designated feeding areas, mentioning that stray dogs shall only be fed in such areas. Under no condition shall the feeding of stray dogs on the streets be permitted. The persons found feeding the dogs on the streets in contravention of the above directions shall be liable to be proceeded against under the relevant legal framework." The stated reason is "reports regarding untoward incidents caused by unregulated feeding" and the aim "to ensure that the practice of feeding dogs on roads and in public places is eliminated".
- 33(e): each municipal authority must create a dedicated helpline for reporting violations, and "appropriate measures shall be taken against the individuals/NGOs concerned".
- 33(f): no individual or organisation may obstruct implementation. Anyone who obstructs a public servant "shall be liable to face prosecution for obstructing the public servant acting in discharge of official duty".
- 33(g): each individual dog lover and NGO that approached the Court had to deposit Rs 25,000 or Rs 2,00,000 respectively within 7 days, failing which they could not appear further. The money goes to dog infrastructure.
- 33(h): animal lovers may apply to the municipal body to adopt street dogs, which are then tagged. It is the adopter's responsibility "to ensure that the adopted stray dogs do not return to the streets".
- Paras 34–36: because the ABC Rules apply uniformly, the scope was expanded "beyond the confines of New Delhi and the NCR region". All States and UTs were impleaded, and pending High Court petitions were transferred to the SC.

**7 Nov 2025 order (same bench; Mehta J.)** — [SC order PDF, 7 Nov 2025](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/41706/41706_2025_3_301_65687_FinalOrder_07-Nov-2025.pdf); operative paras reproduced in the [19 May 2026 judgment, para 5](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/41706/41706_2025_2_1501_71480_Judgement_19-May-2026.pdf)
- The order records that the 22 Aug directions (including "creation of designated feeding zones") "were extended to all States and Union Territories" (paras 2–3). The 19 May 2026 judgment calls the 7 Nov directions "uniform and binding pan-India directions" (para 3).
- Para 25(A): within two weeks, identify all government and private educational institutions, hospitals (including district hospitals, PHCs and medical colleges), public sports complexes, bus stands and depots (including ISBTs) and railway stations.
- 25(B): fencing, boundary walls and gates to keep dogs out, preferably within 8 weeks. 25(C): a Nodal Officer at each institution, with details displayed at the entrance. 25(D): municipal or panchayat inspection at least once every three months.
- 25(E), verbatim in part: the municipal body must "forthwith remove every stray dog found within the premises of an educational institution, hospital (public or private), sports complex, bus stand/depot (including Inter-State Bus Terminal) or railway station and to shift such animal/s to a designated shelter, after due sterilisation and vaccination ... The stray dogs so picked up shall not be released back to the same location from which they were picked up."
- 25(F): stock of anti-rabies vaccine and immunoglobulin in all hospitals. 25(G): awareness sessions in schools. 25(I): waste management at transport hubs "to eliminate food sources that attract animals". 25(J): AWBI SOPs within four weeks.
- Part II reaffirmed the Rajasthan HC's direction of 11 Aug 2025 to remove cattle and stray animals from highways and expressways, with highway patrols and helplines, "implemented uniformly across India".
- Correction for the explainer: some media say the 7 Nov 2025 order "prohibited public feeding" — [Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/india/2025/11/07/stray-dogs-removal-supreme-court.html). In the text I read, the street-feeding ban is in the 22 Aug order, para 33(d). The 7 Nov order refers back to the designated feeding zones and adds the institutional-premises directions. The order notes that institutional venues are vulnerable "if feeding and perimeter management are not enforced".
- 3 Nov 2025: the Court reportedly took serious note of government employees feeding dogs inside office premises, against its designated-zone directions — reported via search summary, not verified in an order text.

**Hearings Jan 2026 (oral remarks, not orders)**
- On 7 Jan 2026 the bench (Nath, Mehta, Anjaria JJ.) resumed hearing the applications against the 7 Nov order — [search summary of LawBeat / The Federal reports](https://lawbeat.in/top-stories/people-are-being-bitten-dying-supreme-court-raps-states-hears-victims-and-animal-rights-groups-on-stray-dog-menace-1554052)
- On 13 Jan 2026 the bench said it would ask States to pay heavy compensation for dog bites and that "dog feeders will also be held responsible and accountable for dog-bite incidents". It asked whether "compassion is limited to animals and not extended to humans" — [All India Radio News, 13 Jan 2026](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/supreme-court-questions-feeding-of-stray-dogs-in-public-places)
- Judgment was reportedly reserved on 29 January 2026 — [policycollapse.in](https://policycollapse.in/news/law/2026/05/supreme-court-issues-judgment-in-suo-motu-stray-dog-case-revisiting-earlier-directions.html) (low-reliability source, unverified)

**19 May 2026 judgment (2026 INSC 506; Mehta J. for Nath, Mehta, Anjaria JJ.)** — [SC judgment PDF, 19 May 2026](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/41706/41706_2025_2_1501_71480_Judgement_19-May-2026.pdf); news report: [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/breaking-dog-bite-menace-rising-supreme-court-refuses-to-modify-directions-to-remove-stray-dogs-from-public-places-534732)
- Para 75: the PCA Act and ABC Rules do not "mandate ... or justify the continued presence or re-release of stray dogs within institutional premises or other restricted-access areas". Para 76: all applications to modify, clarify, vacate, recall or stay the 7 Nov directions "stand dismissed".
- Para 85: all challenges to the AWBI SOPs of 27 Nov 2025 dismissed.
- Para 98: officials who fail to comply face "contempt of Court, disciplinary proceedings and tortious liability".
- Para 101: when human safety is weighed against animal welfare, "the constitutional balance must necessarily and unequivocally tilt in favour of the preservation and protection of human life".
- Para 108(B): at least one fully functional ABC Centre in each district. 108(C): strict compliance with the AWBI SOPs. 108(D): States, UTs and the Union to decide on extending the directions to other high-footfall public spaces.
- 108(H), verbatim in part: where dog numbers are alarming and bites frequent, authorities may, "subject to due assessment by qualified veterinary experts and strictly in accordance with" the PCA Act and ABC Rules, "take such measures as may be legally permissible, including euthanasia in cases involving rabid, incurably ill or demonstrably dangerous/aggressive dogs".
- 108(I): officials acting in good faith under these orders are protected. "No First Information Report, criminal complaint or coercive proceedings shall ordinarily be initiated" against them, except for prima facie mala fides or gross abuse. This bears on feeders who file complaints against dog-catching staff.
- Para 109: all authorities must ensure "strict and faithful compliance with the directions issued by this Court vide orders dated 22nd August, 2025 and 7th November, 2025". **So the street-feeding ban and the designated feeding spaces of 22 Aug 2025 para 33(d) stay in force.**
- Para 111: every High Court must register a suo motu writ petition titled "In Re: Compliance with the directions issued by the Supreme Court in Suo Motu Writ Petition (Civil) No(s). 5 of 2025", before a Division Bench, as a continuing mandamus. High Courts may "expand or tailor" the directions to local conditions "without in any manner diluting" them, and may start contempt proceedings. Chief Secretaries had to file compliance affidavits before the High Courts by 7 Aug 2026. The High Courts report to the SC every four months, with the first consolidated report due a week before **17 Nov 2026**.
- The compliance format in the Annexure, item 26, asks each State and UT to report "Creation of dedicated feeding spaces for stray dogs in each municipal ward". Item 27 asks about the "Mechanism for adoption of stray dogs".
- The Delhi HC registered its suo motu compliance PIL (reported around July 2026) — [India Legal](https://indialegallive.com/constitutional-law-news/courts-news/delhi-high-court-initiates-suo-motu-pil-to-monitor-compliance-with-supreme-court-directions-on-stray-dog-removal/)

### Inferences
- Nationwide vs Delhi-NCR: the 11 Aug 2025 directions were Delhi-NCR only and are now superseded. The 22 Aug 2025 directions, including the feeding ban, were issued while the case was framed around Delhi-NCR, but the same order expanded the case to all States. The 7 Nov 2025 order and the 19 May 2026 judgment (paras 109 and 111, and the Annexure) treat them as binding on every State and UT. Explainer wording: "Since the Supreme Court's 2025 orders, reaffirmed on 19 May 2026, feeding community dogs on streets is not permitted anywhere in India; feeding is allowed only at designated feeding spots that municipal bodies must create in each ward."
- Practical gap: if a ward has no designated spot yet, the text gives no exception. Feeders are exposed to "the relevant legal framework", which the order does not name.

### Gaps
- Outcome of the 17 Nov 2026 SC listing: I found no coverage as of 18 Nov 2026. Check LiveLaw or Bar & Bench before publishing.
- Names of the 11 Aug 2025 two-judge bench: the orders I read only say "two-Judge bench". Widely reported as J.B. Pardiwala and R. Mahadevan JJ., but not verified in a source fetched here.
- Which State or High Court has added local variations under para 111(C) (beyond Delhi starting its PIL) was not researched.
- The 11 Aug 2025 order text itself (para 12(i)–(v) and para 13) was not read.

## Q5. Are feeders liable for bites or "nuisance"? Court rulings on feeder liability

### Takeaway
No court has held, as a general rule, that a feeder is liable in damages for a bite by a street dog. The case law runs the other way: Kerala HC rulings make the local body or panchayat liable to compensate victims. The binding exception is now the SC's 19 May 2026 direction: a group or body that feeds or maintains dogs inside an educational campus must file an affidavit accepting tortious liability, or stop. The SC has repeatedly said orally (Sept 2022, Jan 2026) that feeders may be held accountable, but these were remarks, not holdings. Street feeding outside designated spots can now lead to action under the 22 Aug 2025 order.

### Cited Findings
- **SC 19 May 2026, para 74 (binding direction)**: for animal welfare groups or student-led bodies in educational institutions, "it shall be mandatory for any such group or body operating within such campuses to expressly undertake such liability by filing an affidavit to this effect with the Head of the Institution concerned, failing which no such activity of maintaining or feeding stray dogs shall be permitted within the institutional premises. Failure to comply would entail suitable action against the Head of the Institution concerned." Para 73: NALSAR's Animal Law Centre may run CSVR on its campus experimentally, only after giving an undertaking to accept tortious liability for any bite on campus. Paras 71–72: the Court asked whether those asserting a right to feed would accept "tortious liability" for harm caused by those dogs — [SC judgment PDF](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/41706/41706_2025_2_1501_71480_Judgement_19-May-2026.pdf)
- SC on 13 Jan 2026 (oral remark): "dog feeders will also be held responsible and accountable for dog-bite incidents" — [All India Radio News](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/supreme-court-questions-feeding-of-stray-dogs-in-public-places); also [LiveLaw: "Will Hold Authorities & Dog Feeders Liable For Every Stray Dog Attack"](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/will-hold-authorities-dog-feeders-liable-for-every-stray-dog-attack-says-supreme-court-518667) (headline only, not fetched)
- SC, Sept 2022 (Sanjiv Khanna and J.K. Maheshwari JJ., Kerala stray-dog appeals; oral): those who feed stray dogs "may be held accountable" if the dogs attack, and could perhaps take responsibility for them. One outlet adds that feeders could bear vaccination costs, but this is unconfirmed — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/legal/if-stray-dogs-attack-people-those-who-feed-them-could-be-held-liable-supreme-court); [Moneylife: "We Were Not Sarcastic..."](https://moneylife.in/article/we-were-not-sarcastic-about-making-dogfeeders-liable-for-stray-dog-attacks-supreme-court/79430.html) (headline only)
- **Bombay HC Nagpur bench, Vijay Shankarrao Talewar v State of Maharashtra (Oct 2022)**: banned public feeding of strays in Nagpur, allowed fines, and said people who want to feed should adopt the dogs or keep them in shelters. **The SC stayed this on 16 Nov 2022** in Swati Sudhirchandra Chatterjee v Vijay Shankarrao Talewar (Sanjiv Khanna and J.K. Maheshwari JJ.). It stayed the adoption observation, directed "no coercive steps" including fines for feeding, and asked the Nagpur Municipal Corporation to identify feeding spots and the public to ensure feeding does not cause nuisance. The Court said "Street dogs cannot be kept in captivity" — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/supreme-court-says-no-fine-can-imposed-feeding-stray-dogs-stays-bombay-high-court-observations); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/supreme-court-stays-bombay-hc-observation-that-people-who-feed-street-dogs-must-adopt-them-214255); HC judgment: [Taxguru PDF](https://taxguru.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Vijay-Shankarrao-Talewar-Vs-State-of-Maharashtra-Bombay-High-Court.pdf)
- **Kerala HC (July 2026)**: a Grama Panchayat can be liable to compensate a victim where the attack was a reasonably foreseeable result of its failure to meet statutory duties under the Kerala Panchayat Raj Act and the PCA Act — [LiveLaw, July 2026](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/kerala-high-court/panchayats-liable-breach-statutory-duty-stray-dog-attack-cases-543661); judgment of 28 Jul 2026 in RSA 16/2018: [LiveLaw PDF](https://www.livelaw.in/pdf_upload/2026/07/30/judgment-dated-28072026-in-rsa-16-of-2018-690554.pdf). Earlier rulings: Porathissery Grama Panchayat v Anthony (2016) and Corporation of Calicut (2007) — [LiveLaw (2016)](https://www.livelaw.in/panchayath-must-pay-compensation-victims-stray-dog-bites-kerala-high-court). In a July 2025 interim order, the Kerala HC directed district committees to decide bite-compensation claims — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/kerala-high-court-seeks-info-on-stray-dog-bites-forms-district-bodies-to-process-compensation-claims)
- Officials can also face "tortious liability" for non-compliance — [SC judgment 19 May 2026, para 98](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/41706/41706_2025_2_1501_71480_Judgement_19-May-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Nov 2022 SC stay ("no coercive steps, including fines" for feeding) has in practice been overtaken by the 22 Aug 2025 order, which permits action against people feeding on streets. ## Q6. Can an RWA ban feeding or fine feeders? What remedies do feeders have?

### Takeaway
Rule 20 lets an RWA or AOA fix mutually agreed spots and times and frame guidelines that designated feeders must follow. It does not give the RWA power to ban feeding or impose fines; disputes go to the Animal Welfare Committee and then the State Board (the Bombay HC in 2024 enforced exactly this route). The SC stayed fines on feeders in Nov 2022, but its 22 Aug 2025 order allows action against street feeding outside designated spots. A feeder's remedies are: a rule 20(2) committee and a 20(3) appeal; a complaint to AWBI or the State Board; and a police complaint for intimidation or assault. Feeders must not obstruct municipal dog-catching under the SC orders, and officials acting in good faith under those orders are shielded from FIRs.
