# Animal cruelty law in India (as of 18 Nov 2026): fact-check notes

Access note: indiacode.nic.in, dahd.gov.in PDFs and devgan.in returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool, so official India Code text could not be read directly. Statute wording below comes from the animallaw.info transcription of the PCA Act (Michigan State University College of Law), Indian Kanoon (BNSS) and secondary legal sites. Before publishing, check the wording against the India Code PDF in a browser: https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/11237 (PCA Act).

## 1. PCA Act 1960, section 11: offences, penalties, exceptions; amendment status

### Takeaway
Section 11(1)(a) to (o) is unchanged. The penalty is still a fine of Rs 10 to 50 for a first offence. For a second or later offence within three years, it is a fine of Rs 25 to 100, or up to 3 months in prison, or both. The draft PCA (Amendment) Bill 2022 has **not** been introduced in Parliament: in a written reply on 3 Feb 2026 the government said it was "under examination". It is **not in force**.

### Cited Findings
- **s.11(1) offences** (wording from the transcription) — [animallaw.info, PCA Act 1960 text](https://www.animallaw.info/statute/cruelty-prevention-cruelty-animals-act-1960):
  - (a) beats, kicks, over-rides, over-drives, over-loads, tortures or otherwise treats any animal so as to cause unnecessary pain or suffering; or, as owner, permits this.
  - (b) uses an animal for work or labour when age, disease, infirmity, wound, sore or another cause makes it unfit; or, as owner, permits this.
  - (c) wilfully and unreasonably gives an animal an injurious drug or substance.
  - (d) carries an animal, in a vehicle or not, in a way that causes unnecessary pain or suffering.
  - (e) keeps an animal in a cage or other container too small for reasonable movement.
  - (f) keeps an animal chained or tethered for an unreasonable time on an unreasonably short or heavy chain or cord.
  - (g) as owner, fails to exercise a dog that is habitually chained up or kept in close confinement.
  - (h) as owner, fails to provide sufficient food, drink or shelter.
  - (i) abandons an animal without reasonable cause where it is likely to suffer from starvation or thirst.
  - (j) as owner, wilfully lets a contagiously diseased animal go at large in a street, or lets a diseased or disabled animal die in a street.
  - (k) offers for sale, or keeps without reasonable cause, an animal in pain from mutilation, starvation, thirst, overcrowding or other ill-treatment.
  - (l) mutilates or kills any animal (including stray dogs) by strychnine injection in the heart or in any other unnecessarily cruel manner.
  - (m) solely for entertainment, confines an animal so that it becomes prey for another animal (including tying bait in a sanctuary), or incites animals to fight or bait one another.
  - (n) organises, keeps, uses or manages a place for animal fighting or baiting, or lets it be used, or takes admission money.
  - (o) promotes or takes part in a shooting match or competition in which animals are released from captivity to be shot.
- **Penalty (text):** "in the case of a first offence, with fine which shall not be less than ten rupees but which may extend to fifty rupees and in the case of a second or subsequent offence committed within three years of the previous offence, with fine which shall not be less than twenty-five rupees but which may extend to one hundred rupees or with imprisonment for a term which may extend to three months or with both" — [animallaw.info](https://www.animallaw.info/statute/cruelty-prevention-cruelty-animals-act-1960). Note: the second-offence rule applies only "within three years" of the previous offence, and the planned summary leaves this out.
- **s.11(2):** an owner is deemed to have committed an offence if he failed to exercise reasonable care and supervision to prevent it. An owner convicted only on that ground cannot be imprisoned without the option of a fine — [animallaw.info](https://www.animallaw.info/statute/cruelty-prevention-cruelty-animals-act-1960).
- **s.11(3) exceptions:** section 11 does not apply to:
  - (a) dehorning, castration, branding or nose-roping done in the prescribed manner;
  - (b) destroying stray dogs in lethal chambers or by other prescribed methods;
  - (c) exterminating or destroying any animal under the authority of any law;
  - (d) matters covered by Chapter IV (experiments on animals);
  - (e) killing an animal for food, or preparing to, unless it involves unnecessary pain or suffering.
  - Source: [animallaw.info](https://www.animallaw.info/statute/cruelty-prevention-cruelty-animals-act-1960).
- **Draft Bill 2022:** published for public comment on 21 Nov 2022, with comments due by 7 Dec 2022 — [The Leaflet, 18 Jul 2023](https://theleaflet.in/environment/will-india-finally-update-its-prevention-of-cruelty-to-animals-act); [Down To Earth, 13 Feb 2026](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/governance/sc-street-dog-case-will-the-highest-court-hold-the-authorities-accountable). Down To Earth says more than 7,000 individuals and organisations sent comments.
- **Draft provisions (proposed only):**
  - a new category of "gruesome cruelty": an act causing extreme pain and suffering that may lead to lifelong disability or death, with bestiality included. Penalty: fine of Rs 50,000 to 75,000 (or the cost fixed by a magistrate, whichever is higher) and/or 1 to 3 years' imprisonment.
  - killing an animal: up to 5 years' imprisonment plus a fine.
  - a new s.3A, the "five freedoms" duty.
  - local governments responsible for community animals.
  - Source: [Drishti IAS, 24 Nov 2022](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/draft-prevention-of-cruelty-to-animal-amendment-bill-2022/print_manually).
  - Reports differ on the gruesome-cruelty fine: some say a minimum of Rs 75,000. A March 2026 blog puts the proposed basic first-offence fine at Rs 1,000 to 2,500 per animal; this figure is not checked against the draft text — [indiaisus.com](https://www.indiaisus.com/blog/animal-welfare-india-key-issues-policy-shifts.html).
  - Wikipedia says the draft would make many offences cognizable — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prevention_of_Cruelty_to_Animals_Act,_1960).
- **Status in 2026:** Unstarred Question 504, answered on 3 Feb 2026 (asked by Dr Byreddy Shabari and Shri Appalnaidu Kalisetti; answered by the Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying). The reply said "the proposed bill is under examination". The authors describe the Bill as stalled in the ministry since 2022 — [Down To Earth, 13 Feb 2026](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/governance/sc-street-dog-case-will-the-highest-court-hold-the-authorities-accountable). The names of the two MPs suggest Lok Sabha.
- An earlier PIB written reply (Minister Parshottam Rupala) said the government recognised the need for more stringent penalties — [PIB PRID 1808309](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1808309).
- **State law:** the Kerala Assembly passed the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (Kerala Amendment) Bill 2025 on 9 Oct 2025. It adds a "cattle race" definition, so it is about cattle races, not penalties — [Kerala Niyamasabha, bill as passed](https://niyamasabha.org/codes/15kla/bills/passed/2025%20Prevention%20of%20Cruelty%20to%20Animals%20%20As%20Passed.pdf). Whether it has received assent is not confirmed.

### Inferences
- The figures in the planned page are correct. Add "committed within three years of the previous offence" to the second-offence rule.
- Present any 2022 draft figures as "proposed, not law". No central amendment had been enacted or introduced up to Feb 2026, and the searches found nothing that changes this up to Nov 2026.

### Gaps
- Could not open the official India Code copy or the Lok Sabha/PIB text of the 3 Feb 2026 reply; the reply was confirmed only through Down To Earth.
- The official text of the 2022 draft was not read, so the penalty figures come from secondary sources and vary.
- Nothing reliable found on any central bill activity from Mar to Nov 2026.

## 2. Other PCA sections: 3, 12, 31, 35, 38

### Takeaway
- s.3 puts a general duty of care on anyone in charge of an animal.
- s.12 (phooka or doom dev) is punishable with a fine of up to Rs 1,000, or up to 2 years in prison, or both.
- s.31 makes only 11(1)(l), (n), (o) and s.12 cognizable. All other s.11 offences are non-cognizable under the PCA Act.

### Cited Findings
- **s.3:** every person having the care or charge of an animal must "take all reasonable measures to ensure the well-being of such animal and to prevent the infliction upon such animal of unnecessary pain or suffering" — [animallaw.info](https://www.animallaw.info/statute/cruelty-prevention-cruelty-animals-act-1960).
- **s.12:** performing phooka or doom dev on a cow or other milch animal, or any operation to improve lactation that harms the animal's health, or permitting it. Penalty: fine of up to Rs 1,000, or up to 2 years' imprisonment, or both. The animal is forfeited to the Government — [animallaw.info](https://www.animallaw.info/statute/cruelty-prevention-cruelty-animals-act-1960).
- **s.31:** an offence under clause (l), (n) or (o) of s.11(1), or under s.12, is cognizable "notwithstanding anything contained in the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898" — [animallaw.info](https://www.animallaw.info/statute/cruelty-prevention-cruelty-animals-act-1960). The transcription shows "clause (1)", which is an OCR error for clause (l).
- A March 2026 source says that, because most PCA offences are non-cognizable, serious cases are often prosecuted under criminal law — [indiaisus.com](https://www.indiaisus.com/blog/animal-welfare-india-key-issues-policy-shifts.html).
- **s.35:** the State may name infirmaries to treat animals during cruelty proceedings. A magistrate can order:
  - treatment of the animal;
  - its transfer to a pinjrapole;
  - its destruction, if a veterinary officer certifies that it cannot recover.
  - Costs are paid first by the district magistrate or police commissioner, and the owner can be made to pay them back — [animallaw.info](https://www.animallaw.info/statute/cruelty-prevention-cruelty-animals-act-1960).
- **s.38:** the Centre makes rules by notification after prior publication. Topics include load limits, transport, capture and farrier licensing. Breaking a rule is punishable with a fine of up to Rs 100, or up to 3 months in prison, or both — [animallaw.info](https://www.animallaw.info/statute/cruelty-prevention-cruelty-animals-act-1960).
- **Fines:** AWBI's 2013 advisory on how PCA fines are applied — [AWBI circular, 11 Nov 2013](https://awbi.gov.in/uploads/circular/178576337305Advisory%20PCA%20(Application%20of%20Fines)_11.11.2013.pdf). Not read.

### Inferences
- Rules made under s.38 include the SPCA Rules 2001 and 2023, the ABC (Animal Birth Control) Dog Rules 2023 and the transport rules. For "how to complain", s.31 explains why police often register a case under BNS 325 together with PCA 11: BNS 325 is cognizable, while most of s.11 is not.

### Gaps
- Checked against the animallaw.info transcription only, not India Code.

## 3. BNS 2023, section 325

### Takeaway
BNS 325 reads: "Whoever commits mischief by killing, poisoning, maiming or rendering useless any animal shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to five years, or with fine, or with both." It replaced IPC 428 and 429 from 1 July 2024. Secondary sources say it is cognizable, bailable and triable by a Magistrate of the first class.

### Cited Findings
- **Text and penalty:** as quoted above, up to 5 years or fine or both — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/legal/attention-animal-lovers-now-section-325-of-bharatiya-nyaya-sanhita-considers-animal-related-provisions); [apnilaw bare act](https://www.apnilaw.com/bare-act/bns/section-325-bharatiya-nyaya-sanhita-bns-mischief-by-killing-or-maiming-animal).
- Replaces IPC 428/429. The penalty is now the same whatever the animal or its value, unlike under the IPC — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/legal/attention-animal-lovers-now-section-325-of-bharatiya-nyaya-sanhita-considers-animal-related-provisions).
- **BNS s.2(2):** "animal" means any living creature other than a human being — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/legal/attention-animal-lovers-now-section-325-of-bharatiya-nyaya-sanhita-considers-animal-related-provisions).
- **Classification:** cognizable, bailable, triable by a Magistrate of the first class — [devgan.in BNS 325](https://devgan.in/bns/section/325/) (search snippet; the page itself was blocked).
- Cognizable — [PETA India, Palghar case](https://www.petaindia.com/?p=78670).

### Inferences
- The 1 July 2024 start date of BNS, BNSS and BSA is well established; no source was fetched for it here.

## 5. Article 51A(g) and Supreme Court rulings

### Takeaway
In *AWBI v A. Nagaraja* (7 May 2014, (2014) 7 SCC 547), the Supreme Court:
- read the five freedoms into PCA ss.3 and 11;
- relied on Article 51A(g) and Article 21;
- banned jallikattu and bullock-cart races.

In May 2023 a Constitution Bench upheld the 2017 state laws that allowed jallikattu. Nagaraja is therefore partly superseded, and its jallikattu holding no longer applies.

### Cited Findings
- **Nagaraja:** Civil Appeal 5387 of 2014. The court said the five freedoms are to be read into ss.3 and 11 and "protected and safeguarded" by the States, the Centre, the Union Territories, MoEF and AWBI. It drew on the World Organisation for Animal Health guidelines — [judgment PDF](https://nonhumanrights.org/content/uploads/Animal-Welfare-Board-v-A.-Nagaraja-7.5.2014.pdf); [Law Times Journal](https://lawtimesjournal.in/animal-welfare-board-of-india-vs-a-nagaraja/).
- **Article 51A(g):** inserted by the 42nd Amendment. It is a fundamental duty to protect the natural environment, including wildlife, and to have compassion for living creatures — [Law Times Journal](https://lawtimesjournal.in/animal-welfare-board-of-india-vs-a-nagaraja/). The full wording comes from general knowledge and should be checked against the Constitution text.
- **18 May 2023, AWBI v Union of India:** a five-judge Constitution Bench upheld Tamil Nadu's 2017 amendment and the related state laws allowing jallikattu — [SC judgment PDF](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2016/1216/1216_2016_3_1501_44624_Judgement_18-May-2023.pdf); [The Leaflet](https://theleaflet.in/environment/the-what-why-and-how-of-the-jalikattu-judgment-by-the-supreme-court). Sources disagree on how far the 2023 ruling goes on Article 21 rights for bulls.
- **Stray dogs, Down To Earth:** on 11 Aug 2025 a two-judge bench (suo motu case) ordered dogs removed from Delhi NCR. On 22 Aug 2025 a special bench set that order aside. A 7 Nov order dealt with dogs on institutional premises — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/governance/sc-street-dog-case-will-the-highest-court-hold-the-authorities-accountable).
- **Stray dogs, 19 May 2026:** a three-judge bench reportedly issued nationwide directions allowing euthanasia only for rabid, incurably ill or dangerous dogs — [indiaisus.com](https://www.indiaisus.com/blog/animal-welfare-india-key-issues-policy-shifts.html). This is a single blog source, unverified.

### Inferences
- The explainer can cite Nagaraja for the five freedoms. It should also say that the 2023 Constitution Bench allowed jallikattu under state law.

### Gaps
- The 19 May 2026 order is not confirmed by a primary source or by Live Law or Bar & Bench.

## 6. How to report

### Takeaway
1. File an FIR at any police station (zero FIR) or electronically (e-FIR) under BNSS s.173(1), usually citing BNS 325 together with PCA s.11.
2. If the police refuse, send the substance in writing by post to the SP under s.173(4).
3. If that fails, apply to the Magistrate under s.175(3), with an affidavit.

AWBI also has an online cruelty complaint form. District SPCAs, chaired by the District Collector or District Magistrate, exist under the SPCA Rules.

### Cited Findings
- **s.173(1):** information about a cognizable offence can be given orally or by electronic communication to any police station, "irrespective of the area where the offence is committed". Electronic information must be signed by the informant within 3 days — [Indian Kanoon, BNSS s.173](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/165794322/).
- **s.173(2):** the informant gets a copy free of cost, immediately — [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/165794322/).
- **s.173(3):** for offences punishable with 3 to 7 years, the officer may hold a preliminary enquiry within 14 days, with DSP permission — [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/165794322/). BNS 325 (up to 5 years) falls in this band, so the police can use the preliminary enquiry.
- **s.173(4):** if the station refuses, the person may send the substance in writing by post to the SP. The SP may investigate or direct an investigation. If not, the person may apply to the Magistrate — [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/165794322/).
- **s.175(3):** a Magistrate empowered under s.210 may order investigation "after considering the application supported by an affidavit" made under s.173(4), the police officer's submissions and any enquiry — [Indian Kanoon, BNSS s.175](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/25110765/).
- **Non-cognizable offences (s.174):** police record them in a register and refer the informant to a Magistrate. They cannot investigate without the Magistrate's order — [Finology](https://blog.finology.in/Legal-news/Provision-of-Zero-FIR-E-FIR-under-BNSS).
- **AWBI:** online services "Grievance" (https://awbi.gov.in/cruelty-complaint) and "Cruelty" (https://awbi.gov.in/Cruelty). Contact: support@awbi.gov.in, phone 0129-2555700, 42 Milestone, Delhi-Agra Highway, Ballabhgarh, Faridabad 121004; Mon to Fri, 9:00 to 17:30 — [awbi.gov.in](https://awbi.gov.in/). AWBI also has SPCA and State Animal Welfare Board pages: https://awbi.gov.in/view/index/society-for-prevention-of-cruelty-to-animals and https://awbi.gov.in/view/index/state-animal-welfare-boards.
- **SPCAs:**
  - The 2001 Rules require every state to set up an SPCA in every district, with the District Collector as chairman — [Puducherry gazette 24 Sep 2020](https://styandptg.py.gov.in/2020/SEPTEMBER/EXTRAORDINARYPART-I/136-PART-I%20dated%2024-9-2020.pdf).
  - The SPCA Rules 2023 now govern SPCAs, and the DM or Collector registers the SPCA under the Societies Registration Act — [Legitquest, SPCA Rules 2023](https://www.legitquest.com/act/society-for-prevention-of-cruelty-to-animals-rules-2023/D907).
  - AWBI circular on constituting and reconstituting SPCAs, 12 Feb 2026 — [AWBI circular](https://awbi.gov.in/uploads/circular/177728919308Constitution_Reconstitution%20of%20SPCA%2012_02_2026.pdf). Not read.
- **General reporting advice:** keep a record of whom you contacted, the dates and the outcomes, and call the police first when an animal is in immediate danger — [AWI FAQ](https://awionline.org/node/30920) (a US source, general).

### Inferences
- Evidence to keep: dated photos and video, witness names, a vet report or post-mortem, a copy of the FIR or complaint, and a record of every contact. This is general good practice, not a statutory requirement.
- The term "district animal welfare committee" did not appear in any source; the official body is the district SPCA.

### Gaps
- No official MHA or state police page on the e-FIR scope was found. Many states limit e-FIR to certain offences.
- The AWBI complaint forms were not opened.
