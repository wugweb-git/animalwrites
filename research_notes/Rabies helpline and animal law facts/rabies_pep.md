# Rabies post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) in India: verified facts (checked 18 Nov 2026)

Source notes:
- **WHO fact sheet**: "Rabies", who.int. The page showed the date 17 September 2026 when fetched on 18 Nov 2026. https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies
- **NCDC 2015**: NCDC / NRCP, "National Guidelines on Rabies Prophylaxis", 2015 edition (cover reads "NATIONAL CENTRE FOR DISEASE CONTROL ... 2015"). Hosted on the MoHFW Clinical Establishments site: https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf. This is the full guideline text I could read.
- **NCDC 2019 minutes**: minutes of the NCDC "Expert group meeting to review the National Guidelines on Rabies Prophylaxis", 8 January 2019, chaired by Dr N.S. Dharamshaktu (MoHFW). Hosted on rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in: https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf. An NCDC journal article says the guidance was updated in 2019 ([NCDC Quarterly Journal](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/NCDC-Quarterly-Journal-with-QR-Code-55-60.pdf)). Its wording is therefore taken from the 2015 text plus the 2019 minutes.

## 1. WHO exposure categories I, II, III

### Takeaway
WHO: Category I needs no PEP, Category II needs vaccine, Category III needs vaccine plus RIG or monoclonal antibodies. India uses the same three categories, with two differences: it gives RIG to every Category III patient (it rejected WHO's prioritisation), and it also gives RIG for Category II exposures in immunocompromised people.

### Cited Findings
- WHO Category I: "touching or feeding animals, animal licks on intact skin (no exposure)". Management: "Washing of exposed skin surfaces, no PEP". [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)
- WHO Category II: "nibbling of uncovered skin, minor scratches or abrasions without bleeding (exposure)". Management: "Wound washing and immediate vaccination". [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)
- WHO Category III: "single or multiple transdermal bites or scratches", contamination of mucous membranes or broken skin with saliva from licks, and "exposures due to direct contact with bats (severe exposure)". Management: "Wound washing, immediate vaccination and administration of rabies immunoglobulin/monoclonal antibodies". [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)
- India (2015): "RIG should be administered to all category III exposures. However, in immune compromised individuals, RIG should be administered in both category II and III exposures." [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2015): "All animal bite victims of Category II and III exposures irrespective of age and body weight require the same number of injections and dose per injection." [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2019): "The group did not accept WHO recommendation on prioritization of Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG) in category III exposures. It is recommended that all the patients with Cat-III exposure RIG should be given." [NCDC 2019 minutes](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)
- India (2015), wild animals: "Bite by all wild animals should be treated as category III exposure." [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2015), bats. This differs from WHO: "Bat rabies has not been conclusively proved in India and hence, at present, exposure to bats does not warrant PEP." WHO puts direct bat contact in Category III. [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf); [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)

### Inferences
- A public page can safely use the WHO category wording. It should add the Indian rules that all Category III patients get RIG, and that immunocompromised Category II patients get RIG too.
- The bat advice conflicts. India's 2015 text says no PEP; WHO says Category III. I did not confirm whether the 2019 update changed the bat wording, so the safest course is to tell readers to see a doctor after any bat contact.

### Gaps
- I could not get the full Indian category table text out of the PDF, because of its two-column layout.

## 2. Wound care

### Takeaway
WHO says wash "for at least 15 minutes" with water and soap. The 2019 Indian expert group says washing is "desirable up to 15 minutes". "About 15 minutes" fits both. India adds running water, an antiseptic after washing, no irritants, avoiding stitches and not applying a dressing.

### Cited Findings
- WHO: "extensive wound washing with water and soap for at least 15 minutes soon after an exposure". The key facts section says to "wash the wound with water and soap for at least 15 minutes". [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)
- India (2019): "Washing of wound(s) is/are desirable up to 15 minutes and should be carried out as soon as possible with soap and water. Since the rabies virus can persist and even multiply at the site of bite for a long time, wound(s) toilet must be performed even if the patient report late. If available, local antiseptic can be used for application at wound site." [NCDC 2019 minutes](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)
- India (2015): "prompt and gentle thorough washing with soap or detergent and flushing the wound(s) with running water. If soap and detergent are not immediately available wash with running water." [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2015), antiseptic: "After thorough washing and drying the wound(s), any one of the available chemical viricidal agents should be applied, such as povidone iodine, alcohol, etc." [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2015), irritants: the guideline lists irritants not to apply, such as chillies, oil, turmeric, lime and salt. The cover poster says "Do not apply chillies, mustard oil or any other irritant on the bite wounds". [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2015), suturing: "Suturing of wound(s) should be avoided as far as possible. If surgically unavoidable, after adequate cleansing, rabies immunoglobulin should be infiltrated in the depth and around the wound(s) and suturing should be delayed by a few hours ... Minimum loose sutures should be applied for arresting the bleeding in life threatening situations." [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2015), cover poster: "Do not apply dressing & Do not get the wound stitched"; "Wash the wound immediately with plenty of soap & water". [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)

### Inferences
- "About 15 minutes" is accurate for India. "At least 15 minutes" matches WHO exactly. Either wording is fine; "at least 15 minutes, under running water with soap" covers both.

### Gaps
- None on substance.

## 3. ARV schedules approved in India

### Takeaway
India's 2019 expert review kept only two PEP regimens: IM Essen, 5 doses on days 0, 3, 7, 14, 28 (one vial per dose); and ID Updated Thai Red Cross, 2-2-2-0-2 (two ID sites of 0.1 ml on days 0, 3, 7 and 28). India did not adopt WHO's 2018 one-week ID regimen or the abbreviated 4-dose IM regimen. It asked for multicentre Indian studies first. I found no later national change.

### Cited Findings
- India (2019): "Current Guidelines on post exposure prophylaxis for Anti Rabies vaccine i.e. intradermal updated Thai Red Cross Schedule (2-2-2-0-2) and Intramuscular Regimen i.e. 4 weeks Essen regimen 1 Vial/Dose (1-1-1-1-1) retained." [NCDC 2019 minutes](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)
- India (2019), on WHO's new regimens: "Considering the lack of evidence with respected to who recommendation in Indian settings chairman recommended to multi-centric studies to generate more evidences." [NCDC 2019 minutes](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)
- India (2015), Essen: "intramuscular administration of five injections, one dose each given on days 0, 3, 7, 14 and 28. Day 0 indicates date of [first dose]". [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2015), ID Updated Thai Red Cross: "injection of 0.1ml of reconstituted vaccine per ID site and on two sites ... on days 0, 3, 7 and 28. The day 0 is the date of first dose administration of anti-rabies vaccine and may not be the date of rabies exposure/animal bite." [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2015): every vaccine batch must have "minimum potency of 2.5IU per IM dose, irrespective of whether the vaccine is administered by IM or ID route". Only vaccines approved by DCGI for ID use may be given by the ID route. [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2019), switching brands or routes: PEP "need not be restarted and regimen to be continued/resumed as per new administration route." [NCDC 2019 minutes](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)
- India (2019), previously vaccinated: "intradermal/intramuscular single site dosages on day 0 and day 3. In such patient's treatment with RIG is not required." This covers (a) people with complete, documented pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) and (b) people re-exposed after a full documented course given "three months before". [NCDC 2019 minutes](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)
- India (2019): "only adequate wound washing would be required in case of re-exposure where animal bite victim has documented proof of complete PEP within last three months." [NCDC 2019 minutes](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)
- India (2019), PrEP: ID 0.1 ml or IM full dose "on day 0, 7 and day 21 or 28". [NCDC 2019 minutes](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)
- India (2015): if a biting dog or cat stays healthy for 10 days, an Essen course may be cut short: "skipping the vaccine dose on day 14 and administering it on day 28". On the ID route, "complete course of vaccination should be given irrespective of status of animal." [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- The WHO fact sheet gives no schedule. It says only that the ID route "reduces the amount of necessary vaccine and number of doses". [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)
- Indian Immunologicals' Abhayrab PF page describes only IM use (0.5 ml deep IM, deltoid in adults, anterolateral thigh in children). [IIL AbhayRab PF](https://www.indimmune.com/?p=1977)

### Inferences
- A public page in India should say "5 doses on days 0, 3, 7, 14 and 28 (IM)" or "4 visits on days 0, 3, 7 and 28 (ID, two sites)". The 1-week 0-3-7 ID regimen and the 4-dose IM regimen should not be presented as Indian national guidance.
- The ID route depends on the brand. Only DCGI-approved ID-labelled vaccines may be used intradermally.

### Gaps
- I did not re-fetch the WHO 2018 position paper (WER 93(16), April 2018) or TRS 1012 in this session. Check the WHO regimens there before quoting them: 1-week 2-site ID on days 0, 3, 7; IM 4-dose 0, 3, 7, 14–28; IM Zagreb 2-1-1; previously vaccinated, days 0 and 3 or 4-site ID on day 0.
- I did not confirm whether any Indian guideline from 2020 to 2026, or the NAPRE document, changed these schedules. None was found in this session.

## 4. Rabies immunoglobulin (RIG) and monoclonal antibodies

### Takeaway
India: HRIG 20 IU/kg, ERIG 40 IU/kg. As much of the dose as possible goes into and around all wounds, with any leftover given IM near the exposure site. The full dose must not be given into the buttock, and the half-wound, half-IM split is not used. RIG can be given up to day 7 after the first vaccine dose. Two rabies monoclonal antibodies are licensed in India: Rabishield (2016) and Twinrab (September 2019). The 2019 expert group still asked for Indian multicentre studies of monoclonals.

### Cited Findings
- India (2015): "The dose of ERIG is 40 IU per kg body weight of patient. The ERIG produced in India contains 300 IU per ml. The dose of the HRIG is 20 IU per kg body weight. HRIG preparation is available in concentration of 150 IU per ml." [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2019): "It is essential to infiltrate RIG as much as possible in all wounds as per maximum calculated dose ... If at all some dose remains to be injected it can be infiltrated intramuscularly ... as close as possible to the presumed exposure site". [NCDC 2019 minutes](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)
- India (2015): "Administration of full dose of RIG intramuscularly into the gluteal region or infiltration of half the dose of RIG locally and half intramuscularly is not recommended." [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2015): RIG "can be administered up to the seventh day after the administration of the first dose of ARV". An infected wound does not rule out infiltration. Patients should be observed for at least half an hour after ERIG, and no skin test is needed ("ERIG should be given irrespective of the result of" a skin test). [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2019): "The role of Monoclonal antibodies in case of category III bites as a replacement to Rabies Immunoglobulin needs to be studied with regards to its effectiveness, Safety in multi-centric Indian settings." [NCDC 2019 minutes](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)
- WHO's Category III management includes "rabies immunoglobulin/monoclonal antibodies", so WHO accepts monoclonals. [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)
- Twinrab is a cocktail of docaravimab and miromavimab, licensed in India in September 2019. A post-marketing study gave a single 40 IU/kg dose. [PMC TwinRab in-use stability](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12759163/); [PMC8993100](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8993100)
- Rabishield is a single human IgG1 monoclonal, licensed in India in 2016. A clinical decision-support pathway (secondary) lists its dose as 3.33 IU/kg and Twinrab's as 40 IU/kg. [Mediscuss CDSS pathway](https://mediscuss.org/cdss/rabies-pep)

### Inferences
- "RIG for every Category III bite, infiltrated into the wound" is correct for India.

### Gaps
- The Rabishield 3.33 IU/kg figure comes only from a secondary pathway. I did not see it on the product label or in an Indian guideline. Check the package insert before publishing.

## 5. Human incubation period

### Takeaway
WHO wording: "typically 2–3 months but may vary from one week to one year". India stresses that because the period is long and variable, people who come late must still be treated.

### Cited Findings
- WHO: "The incubation period for rabies is typically 2–3 months but may vary from one week to one year". [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)
- India (2015): "people who present for treatment even months or years after a possible rabies exposure should be evaluated and treated as if the event had occurred recently." [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)

### Inferences
- The brief's wording ("typically 2–3 months, from 1 week to 1 year") matches WHO.

### Gaps
- None.

## 6. Is there a cure once symptoms appear?

### Takeaway
No. WHO says rabies is "virtually 100% fatal" once clinical symptoms appear. NRCP materials put it as "100 percent fatal but preventable".

### Cited Findings
- WHO: "Once clinical symptoms appear, rabies is virtually 100% fatal." [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)
- The NCDC/NRCP guideline cover poster reads "RABIES IS 100 PERCENT FATAL BUT PREVENTABLE". [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Virtually 100% fatal" is the more precise phrase for a fact-checked page.

### Gaps
- None.

## 7. Rodent, rat, mouse and squirrel bites

### Takeaway
WHO: rodent bites "are not known to transmit rabies". India (2015): "domestic rodents, squirrel, hare and rabbits do not ordinarily require PEP". Wild-animal bites are Category III, and some state guidance asks for expert advice on wild rodents. India's position is broadly in line with WHO and the US CDC. The word "ordinarily" and the domestic/wild split are the Indian caveats.

### Cited Findings
- WHO: "bites from rodents are not known to transmit rabies." [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies)
- India (2015): "Bite by rodents: Exposure to domestic rodents, squirrel, hare and rabbits do not ordinarily require PEP." [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2015): "Bite by all wild animals should be treated as category III exposure." [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- State guidance (a secondary summary of a West Bengal NRCP guideline, October 2019): domestic rodent exposure needs no PEP, a wild rodent bite may need PEP after consulting an expert, and it can be hard to tell whether a large rat is domestic or wild. [West Bengal / pashudhanpraharee PDF](https://www.pashudhanpraharee.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/ANIMAL-BITE-MANAGEMENT-AND-POST-EXPOSURE-PROPHYLAXIS-OF-RABIES.pdf). I read this only through a search summary, not the original PDF.
- A secondary summary says Himachal Pradesh state guidance states that squirrels have been found to transmit rabies. This is not national guidance. Not verified at the primary source.

### Inferences
- Suggested page wording: "Bites from house rats, mice, squirrels and rabbits do not normally need rabies vaccine under India's national guidelines. A doctor should still assess every bite, and bites from wild animals are treated as severe (Category III)."

### Gaps
- The 2019 minutes do not mention rodents, so the 2015 wording presumably still stands. Not confirmed.

## 8. "Does every dog have rabies?" and the 10-day observation period

### Takeaway
India uses a 10-day observation period for dogs and cats only. PEP starts at once and is not delayed for observation. If the animal stays healthy, an IM Essen course may be shortened. I found no authoritative source saying in so many words that "not every dog has rabies". The guideline's logic (start PEP at once, a vaccinated dog can still transmit) supports "you cannot tell by looking, so treat every Category II or III bite".

### Cited Findings
- India (2015): "The PEP should be started immediately after the bite. The observation period of 10 days is valid for dogs and cats only. The natural history of rabies in mammals other than dogs and cats is not fully understood and therefore the 10-day observation period is not applicable." [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2015): "Although unvaccinated animals are more likely to transmit rabies, vaccinated animals can also do so if the vaccination of the biting animal was ineffective for any reason. A history of rabies vaccination in an animal is not always a guarantee that the biting animal [is not rabid]". [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2015), shortening the course if the dog or cat stays healthy for 10 days: covered in section 3 above. [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)
- India (2019): "Consumption of milk produced by rabid animal dose not requires Rabies PEP." [NCDC 2019 minutes](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)

### Inferences
- A fact-safe line: "Most dogs do not have rabies, but you cannot tell by looking, and even a vaccinated dog can occasionally transmit it. Start PEP at once. If the dog or cat stays healthy for 10 days, your doctor may shorten the course."

### Gaps
- I found no WHO or Indian source giving the share of dogs in India that carry rabies, so do not publish a percentage.

## 9. Free ARV and RIG in government facilities, NRCP, NAPRE, recent updates

### Takeaway
ARV and RIG (or anti-rabies serum, ARS) are free at public health facilities under the National Health Mission's (NHM) Free Drugs Service Initiative, and are on the national and state essential drug lists (Health Ministry, August 2025). Human rabies is notifiable state by state, not nationally. MoHFW asked all states and UTs to notify it in 2021. Reported state actions include Karnataka (2022), Odisha (2023) and Delhi (January/February 2026). Secondary sources say about 20 states have done so.

### Cited Findings
- PIB / MoHFW (Parliament reply, August 2025, as summarised by search): "Life-saving drugs such as Anti-Rabies Vaccine (ARV) and Anti-Rabies Serum (ARS)/Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG) are provided free of cost in the public health facilities under National Free Drug Initiative of NHM", and these drugs are on the National and State Essential Drug Lists. [PIB release 2117342](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2117342&reg=48&lang=2); [PIB on X, Aug 2025](https://x.com/PIB_India/status/1957753563683385412). The PIB page returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch, so the wording comes from the search snippet.
- The same coverage mentions NRCP surveillance of animal bites and rabies, training, and model anti-rabies clinics. [PIB on X](https://x.com/PIB_India/status/1957753563683385412)
- 2019 background: states had been told they could buy ARV/ARS with state funds under the free drugs initiative. ERIG stock was then "not sufficient for meeting demand" of Category III cases. [NCDC 2019 minutes](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)
- Notifiable status: MoHFW wrote to all States/UTs on 20 September 2021 asking them to make human rabies notifiable. Delhi announced it in January 2026 under the Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897, with a gazette reportedly dated 6 February 2026. Karnataka acted in 2022 and Odisha in September 2023. A secondary source says "20 States" have notified. [Drishti IAS, 6 Jan 2026](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/rabies-as-notifiable-disease/print_manually); [Business Standard, Jan 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/health/delhi-set-to-end-rabies-deaths-with-tracking-mandatory-case-reporting-126010500339_1.html); [Odisha Bytes](https://odishabytes.com/odisha-government-declares-human-rabies-as-notifiable-disease/); [Kashmir Monitor (J&K)](https://www.thekashmirmonitor.net/jk-declares-human-rabies-a-notifiable-disease/)
- One secondary site says the Supreme Court on 7 November 2025 ordered hospitals to keep mandatory stocks of ARV and immunoglobulin. Unverified. [dogeats.in](https://dogeats.in/stats/anti-rabies-injection-cost-schedule-india/)

### Inferences
- "Free at government hospitals" is supported. "Available at every PHC" is not verified; availability on the ground varies.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the primary NAPRE document (launched 28 Sept 2021; goal of zero human deaths from dog-mediated rabies by 2030). The launch date and goal are general knowledge, not verified this session. Check rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in.
- I did not find the 15400 rabies helpline or an official count of states that have notified rabies.

## 10. Typical private cost in India

### Takeaway
Online pharmacy price for one Abhayrab vial: about ₹268 to ₹358 (late 2026 listings). Private clinics add an administration or consultation charge; secondary sources put a dose at ₹300 to ₹2,000 and a full private course at ₹2,000 to ₹5,000. Price regulator NPPA's ceiling for anti-rabies immunoglobulin (300 IU/ml) is ₹119.48 per ml, excluding GST, revised in 2026. So ERIG for a 60 kg adult (40 IU/kg = 2,400 IU = 8 ml) has a drug ceiling cost of about ₹956 before GST and fees. That figure is my own arithmetic. Consumer sites quote ₹3,000 to ₹9,000 for RIG. All figures are uncertain.

### Cited Findings
- Abhayrab ₹357.76 per vial (Indian Immunologicals Ltd) on 1mg and PharmEasy, and ₹267 on Medikabazaar. These are search-result snippets read November 2026. [1mg Abhayrab](https://www.1mg.com/drugs/abhayrab-vaccine-37993); [PharmEasy Abhayrab PF](https://pharmeasy.in/online-medicine-order/abhayrab-pf-dry-vial-of-1-vaccine-228361); [Medikabazaar](https://www.medikabazaar.com/pharmaceutical/anti-rabies-injection)
- NPPA revised the ceiling price of anti-rabies immunoglobulin 300 IU/ml injection to ₹119.48 per ml, from ₹112.19 per ml (NLEM 2022), excluding GST. The revision followed DoP review order No. 31015/02/2025-Pricing dated 27 March 2026 and was reported 12 July 2026. [Medical Dialogues, 12 Jul 2026](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/nppa-revises-ceiling-price-of-anti-rabies-immunoglobulin-injection-174861)
- Consumer and secondary estimates: ₹800 to ₹2,000 per dose and ₹2,000 to ₹5,000 per course at private hospitals; RIG about ₹3,000 to ₹9,000. [Policybazaar](https://www.policybazaar.com/health-wellness/diseases/rabies/treatment-cost/); [Bajaj Finserv](https://www.bajajfinservmarkets.in/preventive-care/rabies-vaccine.html)
- Pune civic reporting: private hospitals charge ₹300 to ₹400 per injection, and RIG can cost over ₹4,000; municipal hospitals give the vaccine free. [Punekar News](https://www.punekarnews.in/?p=231458)

### Inferences
- A safe line: "In private clinics one vaccine dose usually costs a few hundred rupees and up, and immunoglobulin several thousand. Both are free at government hospitals."

### Gaps

## 11. Abhayrab: maker, type, WHO prequalification, alerts

### Takeaway
Abhayrab is made and marketed by Indian Immunologicals Ltd (IIL), Hyderabad. It is a lyophilised (freeze-dried), purified, inactivated rabies vaccine grown in Vero cells (PVRV). The IIL page I read does not mention "Human Biologicals Institute". Abhayrab does not appear among WHO-prequalified human rabies vaccines; the search found only Rabivax-S, VaxiRab N and Verorab. Counterfeit batch KA24014 was flagged by Delhi Drugs Control (March 2025) and by Australia's ATAGI (19 December 2025). IIL calls it an isolated batch no longer on shelves.

### Cited Findings
- IIL page: AbhayRab PF is a "lyophilized, purified, inactivated Rabies vaccine" containing "L. Pasteur 2061/Vero Strain propagated in Vero cells". The site is run by Indian Immunologicals Ltd. [IIL AbhayRab PF](https://www.indimmune.com/?p=1977)
- WHO prequalified human rabies vaccines found: RABIVAX-S (Serum Institute of India, FVP-P-322), VaxiRab N (Zydus) and VERORAB (Sanofi). Abhayrab was not found. A 2024 source says there were "only 3 WHO pre-qualified human rabies vaccines". [WHO PQ Rabivax-S](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/vaccines/p/rabies-vaccine-inactivated-freeze-driedrabivax-s); [WHO PQ VaxiRab N](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/vaccines/p/vaxirab-n); [Precision Vaccinations](https://precisionvaccinations.com/rabies-vaccines)
- ATAGI (Australia) statement, December 2025, updated January 2026: counterfeit Abhayrab reported in India and said to be circulating since 2023. It advises that travellers who got Abhayrab of that batch, or of an unknown batch, may not be fully protected. [ATAGI statement, Dec 2025](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/atagi-statement-counterfeit-rabies-vaccine-abhayrab-reported-in-india-guidance-for-travellers-and-healthcare-providers.pdf); [ATAGI update, Jan 2026](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-01/atagi-statement-counterfeit-rabies-vaccine-abhayrab-reported-in-india-guidance-for-travellers-and-healthcare-providers.pdf) (the PDF timed out, so details come from news coverage)
- IIL response: the problem is confined to batch KA 24014, which had different packaging. IIL says it reported this to regulators and police, the batch is no longer on shelves, the advisory is "outdated", and it has made Abhayrab since 2000. The Delhi Drugs Control Department issued an advisory on that batch on 23 March 2025, reporting sales in New Delhi, Mumbai, Ahmedabad and Lucknow. [Business Today, 27 Dec 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/fake-rabies-vaccine-iil-says-real-abhayrab-safe-after-australia-alert-calls-it-isolated-case-508368-2025-12-27); [The Week, 28 Dec 2025](https://www.theweek.in/news/health/2025/12/28/australia-flags-fake-rabies-vaccine-in-india-what-is-the-controversy.html); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/health/australia-warns-fake-rabies-vaccines-india-abhayrab-125122600384_1.html); [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/news/hyderabad-vaccine-firm-junks-australian-alert-on-fake-rabies-doses-as-outdated-misleading)

### Inferences
- Do not call Abhayrab "WHO-prequalified". Do not say it is made by "Human Biologicals Institute" unless a current IIL or CDSCO source confirms it. "Indian Immunologicals Ltd" is verified.
- If the page names brands, it could tell readers to buy only from licensed pharmacies or hospitals and to keep the vial label or batch number.

### Gaps
- The Human Biologicals Institute link (historically IIL's human-vaccine division) was not confirmed in any current source.
- I did not find a CDSCO national drug alert for KA24014; only the Delhi state advisory was reported. I found no reports after January 2026.
