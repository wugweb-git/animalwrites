# India's own rules overturn common animal-page claims

Most claims on the Animal Writes health, helpline and law pages hold up, but five need fixing before they are republished. The rabies vaccine schedule must follow India's 2019 national guideline, not WHO's shorter 2018 regimens. The "18,000 to 20,000 rabies deaths a year" figure is about 20 years old; the current peer-reviewed estimate is about 5,700. 1962 is a set of state-run livestock vet lines, not one national stray-animal service. Feeding community dogs on the street has been banned nationwide since the Supreme Court's 22 August 2025 order, and the 19 May 2026 judgment confirmed the ban. NGO Darpan has been rebuilt as "NPO DARPAN", with new menus and no physical certificate. Some things are as the pages say. India's cruelty fines are still Rs 10 to 50, because the 2022 amendment bill has never been introduced. Rabies vaccine and immunoglobulin are free at government facilities, though under the National Health Mission's free-drugs scheme rather than the rabies programme. The two rabies notes files disagree on the guideline edition and on who makes Abhayrab. On the edition, the second check is stronger because it read the full 2019 guideline that NCDC currently hosts. On the maker, both files agree it is Indian Immunologicals Ltd. Several helpline numbers, the 17 November 2026 Supreme Court hearing and a few parliamentary figures could not be confirmed from primary sources. They must be checked by hand. Verdicts below use four labels: **correct**, **reword**, **wrong**, **unverified**.

## Rabies care in India follows the 2019 NCDC guideline, not WHO 2018

### Which rabies notes file to trust

The two notes files reached the same answers on most points but started from different documents. `rabies_pep.md` could not find a full post-2015 national guideline. It therefore used the **2015 NCDC "National Guidelines on Rabies Prophylaxis"** plus the minutes of the 8 January 2019 expert review ([NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf); [NCDC minutes, 8 Jan 2019](https://rabiesfreeindia.mohfw.gov.in/resources/uploads/PageContentPdf/170687215557.pdf)). `rabies_second_check.md` found and read the **"National Guidelines for Rabies Prophylaxis 2019"**, 52 pages, DGHS foreword dated 24 February 2020, now hosted at a new NCDC address after the site was rebuilt. It also found that the older link returns 404 ([NCDC 2019](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf)). **The 2019 guideline is the stronger source** because it is the current consolidated text. Pages should cite it, and any link to the 2015 PDF or the dead 2024 NCDC link should be replaced. The 2019 text settles one question the first file left open: it keeps the rule that bat exposure does not warrant PEP in India. It also changes two wordings. Wild-animal bites become "bites by wild animals and all bites in forest areas" (Category III). The rodent sentence becomes "domestic rodents, hare and rabbits", with a new line that rodent bites in forest areas need PEP. The 2015 text also listed squirrels, and the 2019 quotation as read does not. Pages should not name squirrels unless an editor confirms the 2019 wording.

On Abhayrab's maker, the files agree on the company and differ on the unit. `rabies_pep.md` confirmed only **Indian Immunologicals Ltd (IIL), Hyderabad**, from IIL's own product page, and could not confirm the "Human Biologicals Institute" link ([IIL AbhayRab PF](https://www.indimmune.com/?p=1977)). `rabies_second_check.md` says HBI is an IIL subsidiary, from a September 2026 press report quoting IIL. It also notes that the 2019 guideline's list of approved makers includes "Human Biological Limited, Hyderabad" ([Khaleej Times, 5 Sep 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/asia/india-immunologicals-statement-abhayrab-rabies-vaccine-found-substandard); [NCDC 2019](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf)). The second check has more evidence, but it is secondary or indirect. The safest public line is "made by Indian Immunologicals Ltd". Add "(Human Biologicals Institute unit)" only after checking a current IIL or CDSCO page. The two files also cite **different PIB releases** (PRID 2117342 and PRID 2157880) for the free-vaccine statement. Both were read only through search summaries, so an editor should open both.

| Vaccine schedule: "4 doses over 2 weeks" or a "1-week" intradermal course | **Wrong for India** | India kept IM Essen, 5 doses on days 0, 3, 7, 14, 28, and ID Updated Thai Red Cross, 2 sites on days 0, 3, 7, 28. It did not adopt WHO's 2018 shorter regimens ([NCDC 2019 §4.1.3, §4.2.1](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/resource/1769332447_National-Guidelines-for-Rabies-Prophylaxis.pdf)) |
| Start the vaccine the same day; the course takes about a month | **Correct** | Start "irrespective of time-lapse". Missing a dose by a day or two does not mean restarting (NCDC 2019 ch. 8) |
| RIG dose and timing | **Correct as HRIG 20 IU/kg, ERIG 40 IU/kg** | Infiltrate into and around the wounds. Not indicated after day 7 from the first vaccine dose. Never give the full dose into the buttock (NCDC 2019 §2.9; [NCDC 2015](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/238.pdf)) |
| Monoclonal antibodies replace RIG | **Reword** | WHO accepts them. Rabishield (2016) and Twinrab (2019) are licensed in India. NCDC 2019 says their role "needs to be studied" before they enter national guidance ([PMC8993100](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8993100)) |
| Previously vaccinated people need two doses and no RIG | **Correct** | Days 0 and 3, no RIG, when an earlier full course is documented (NCDC 2019 ch. 5) |
| Symptoms usually appear 2–3 months after a bite, from under a week to over a year | **Reword** | WHO says "typically 2–3 months but may vary from one week to one year". Mirror that wording. Add NCDC's point that people who come months or years later should still be treated |
| No cure once symptoms appear; always or 100% fatal | **Reword "always" to "virtually 100%"** | WHO says "virtually 100% fatal". NCDC 2019 says "almost 100%". A few survivors are documented, so avoid "always" |
| Rat and mouse bites do not need the vaccine | **Reword** | WHO: rodents "are not known to transmit rabies". NCDC 2019: domestic rodents, hares and rabbits do not *ordinarily* need PEP, but rodent bites in forest areas do. Offer PEP when the animal may have been a mongoose or mole. Still wash the wound and check tetanus cover (FAQ 13) |
| Watch the dog for 10 days before deciding | **Wrong** | Start PEP at once. The 10-day watch applies to dogs and cats only. If the animal stays healthy, an IM course can be shortened (NCDC 2019 §2.2, FAQ 9) |
| A vaccinated dog is very unlikely to have rabies | **Reword** | NCDC: vaccination of the animal "does not guarantee" it is not rabid. Give PEP regardless. Do not lead with reassurance (§2.3). No source gives a share of Indian dogs with rabies, so publish no percentage |
| ARV is free at government hospitals under the National Rabies Control Programme | **Reword the mechanism** | ARV **and** RIG are free under the **National Free Drug Initiative of NHM**. NRCP funds training, surveillance, labs and advocacy, not the drugs ([PIB 2157880](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2157880&reg=48&lang=2); [PIB 2117342](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2117342&reg=48&lang=2), both read through search summaries only). An ICMR study found ARV at about 80% of public facilities ([Medical Dialogues](https://health.medicaldialogues.in/health/study-finds-major-gaps-across-indias-rabies-vaccine-availability-151548)). Do not say "every PHC" |

NPPA's ceiling for 300 IU/ml immunoglobulin is ₹119.48/ml before GST, revised in 2026 ([Medical Dialogues, 12 Jul 2026](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/nppa-revises-ceiling-price-of-anti-rabies-immunoglobulin-injection-174861)). Consumer sites put private courses at ₹2,000–5,000 and RIG at ₹3,000–9,000 ([Policybazaar](https://www.policybazaar.com/health-wellness/diseases/rabies/treatment-cost/)). Say "a few hundred rupees a dose and up; immunoglobulin several thousand" |
| Abhayrab is a purified Vero-cell vaccine | **Correct** | IIL: "lyophilized, purified, inactivated", grown in Vero cells ([IIL](https://www.indimmune.com/?p=1977)) |
| Abhayrab is WHO-prequalified | **Wrong (as far as found)** | WHO's prequalified list showed Rabivax-S, VaxiRab N and Verorab, and not Abhayrab ([WHO PQ Rabivax-S](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/vaccines/p/rabies-vaccine-inactivated-freeze-driedrabivax-s)) |
| India has 18,000–20,000 rabies deaths a year; 36% of world deaths | **Wrong as a current figure** | Both trace to a 2004 APCRI survey. ICMR-NIE's 2022–23 survey (Lancet Infectious Diseases, 2024) estimates **5,726 deaths (95% UI 3,967–7,350)** and **9.1 million animal bites** a year ([Amrita record](https://www.amrita.edu/publication/estimates-of-the-burden-of-human-rabies-deaths-and-animal-bites-in-india-2022-23-a-community-based-cross-sectional-survey-and-probability-decision-tree-modelling-study/)). Hospital-reported deaths are only 130–210 a year, because most deaths are never reported (NCDC 2019). WHO's 2026 fact sheet gives 44,203 deaths worldwide and no India share |

The Abhayrab safety story needs careful handling because it involves **two separate incidents**. In the first, counterfeit batch KA24014 was flagged by Delhi Drugs Control on 23 March 2025. Australia's ATAGI then issued a statement in December 2025, updated in January 2026. Victoria Health advised that anyone who got Abhayrab in India **from 1 November 2023** should be offered replacement doses ([Health Victoria](https://health.vic.gov.au/health-alerts/counterfeit-rabies-vaccine-abhayrab-in-india); [ATAGI Jan 2026](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-01/atagi-statement-counterfeit-rabies-vaccine-abhayrab-reported-in-india-guidance-for-travellers-and-healthcare-providers_0.pdf)). IIL calls this an isolated batch that has been removed and calls the advisory outdated ([Business Today, 27 Dec 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/fake-rabies-vaccine-iil-says-real-abhayrab-safe-after-australia-alert-calls-it-isolated-case-508368-2025-12-27)). In the second, in September 2026, CDSCO reported vials of batch KE25012, seized from unlicensed premises in Delhi, as "Not of Standard Quality and misbranded". IIL says HBI did not make them ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/asia/india-immunologicals-statement-abhayrab-rabies-vaccine-found-substandard)). A public page should not merge the two incidents or imply that the brand is unsafe in general. It should tell readers to get vaccinated at a government or trusted clinic and keep the batch details. Two further facts are confirmed. The Supreme Court's 7 November 2025 order (para 25(F)) requires all hospitals to stock ARV and immunoglobulin; `rabies_pep.md` had this only from a weak site, and the order text confirms it. Several states, Delhi most recently in early 2026, have made human rabies a notifiable disease ([Business Standard, Jan 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/health/delhi-set-to-end-rabies-deaths-with-tracking-mandatory-case-reporting-126010500339_1.html)).

## 1962 is a set of state livestock-vet lines, not one national animal ambulance

1962 is a toll-free number that **each State or UT runs** under the Mobile Veterinary Unit component of DAHD's Livestock Health and Disease Control Programme. The Centre pays for the vehicles and shares the running costs. Many states contract the work out: **EMRI Green Health Services** ran 1,581 vehicles in eight states and a UT in December 2025, and a CSR-funded company runs Rajasthan's call centre ([EMRI GHS Dec 2025](http://www.emri.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/EMRI-Green-Health-Services-Monthly-Report-December-25.pdf); [PIB 1823690](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1823690)). The number is older than the central scheme. Tamil Nadu used it from 2016, and Gujarat's Karuna ambulance and Telangana's service from 2017 ([Wikipedia: 1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_(emergency_telephone_number))). The latest national figure is **4,019 MVUs operating in 29 States/UTs**, from a Lok Sabha reply of 12 August 2025. A February 2025 reply gave 4,340 and did not explain the difference. Neither annexure, which lists the states, could be read ([Sansad AU3885](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2995631/1/AU3885_2lUdSR.pdf)).

Say "in some states" |

## Cruelty fines remain Rs 50 because the 2022 bill never reached Parliament

**PCA Act s.11(1)(a)–(o) is unchanged.** A first offence carries a fine of Rs 10 to 50. A second offence within three years carries Rs 25 to 100, or up to 3 months' prison, or both. Pages often leave out the "within three years" condition ([animallaw.info transcription](https://www.animallaw.info/statute/cruelty-prevention-cruelty-animals-act-1960)). The draft PCA (Amendment) Bill 2022 was published for comment in November 2022. In a written reply on 3 February 2026 the ministry said it was still "under examination", so its proposed penalties are not law ([Down To Earth, 13 Feb 2026](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/governance/sc-street-dog-case-will-the-highest-court-hold-the-authorities-accountable)). Those penalties included Rs 50,000–75,000 and/or 1–3 years for "gruesome cruelty", and up to 5 years for killing an animal ([Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/draft-prevention-of-cruelty-to-animal-amendment-bill-2022/print_manually)). Serious cases are prosecuted under **BNS s.325**, which replaced IPC 428 and 429 on 1 July 2024. It punishes killing, poisoning, maiming or rendering useless "any animal" with **up to five years, or fine, or both**, whatever the animal's value. Secondary sources classify it as cognizable and bailable ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/legal/attention-animal-lovers-now-section-325-of-bharatiya-nyaya-sanhita-considers-animal-related-provisions)). The two laws work as a pair because PCA s.31 makes only s.11(1)(l), (n), (o) and s.12 cognizable. Police therefore usually register BNS 325 alongside PCA s.11.

## Street feeding has been banned nationwide since 22 August 2025

The notes disagree on this topic too, and the feeding notes win. `cruelty_law.md` called the 19 May 2026 judgment "a single blog source, unverified". `feeding_law.md` read the Supreme Court PDFs themselves, so it is the stronger source. The controlling text is the **19 May 2026 judgment, 2026 INSC 506**, by Nath, Mehta and Anjaria JJ., in *In Re: "City Hounded by Strays, Kids Pay Price"*, SMWP (C) 5 of 2025. It dismissed every application to modify the earlier orders, upheld AWBI's SOPs of 27 November 2025, and ordered "strict and faithful compliance" with the orders of 22 August and 7 November 2025 ([SC judgment, 19 May 2026](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/41706/41706_2025_2_1501_71480_Judgement_19-May-2026.pdf)).

The 22 August order, para 33(d), is the source of the feeding rule. It says "under no condition shall the feeding of stray dogs on the streets be permitted". Municipal bodies must create **dedicated feeding spaces in each ward**, and people found feeding on the streets are "liable to be proceeded against under the relevant legal framework" ([SC order, 22 Aug 2025](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/41706/41706_2025_3_1501_63567_Order_22-Aug-2025.pdf)). The same order kept the Delhi-NCR no-release direction of 11 August in abeyance and widened the case to all States. The order does not say what happens in a ward that has no designated spot yet. Some media report that the 7 November order "prohibited public feeding". That is inaccurate: the 7 November order removed dogs from schools, hospitals, sports complexes, bus stands and railway stations to shelters, with no re-release ([SC order, 7 Nov 2025](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/41706/41706_2025_3_301_65687_FinalOrder_07-Nov-2025.pdf)).

**ABC Rules 2023, rule 20** still governs feeding inside housing societies. The RWA, AOA or local body arranges feeding together with the feeders, at mutually agreed spots and times away from children's play areas and entrances. Disputes go to an Animal Welfare Committee, whose decision on the feeding point is final, with an appeal to the State Board. The Bombay HC sent a dispute down exactly this route in 2024 ([RNA Royal Park v MCGM, 2 Aug 2024](https://verdictum.in/pdf_upload/rna-royal-park-co-operative-housing-society-v-municipal-corporation-of-greater-mumbaiwatermark-1643078.pdf)).

## Conclusion

Errors on these pages fall into two kinds. Some were never true for India: WHO's 2018 vaccine schedule, the 10-day "wait and watch", 1962 as a national stray service. Others were true once and have since gone out of date: 20,000 rabies deaths, AWBI in Chennai, CPCSEA, NGO Darpan's old menus, and the right to feed on the street. The first kind is fixed by citing Indian primary texts: the 2019 NCDC guideline, the PCA Act, and the Supreme Court orders. The second kind needs dates. Every count and name on these pages should say when it was last checked: MVUs, tigers, dolphins, chairs, helpline numbers. The fastest-moving area is the stray-dog case. Monitoring now sits with every High Court, so state-level rules on feeding spots can diverge from the national text. The feeding explainer will need a "last checked" line and a review after each Supreme Court listing, starting with the outcome of 17 November 2026.

The rabies page should keep reassurance in proportion. The most useful lines, all backed by NCDC, push readers to act: wash for 15 minutes, start the vaccine the same day even if the bite was days ago, finish every dose, and ask for referral if a clinic has run out. The consumer-protection message from the Abhayrab incidents fits the same pattern. Get vaccinated at a government or trusted clinic and keep the batch number, rather than avoiding a brand. All suggested wording in this report still needs the owner's yes under the site copy rule before it goes live.
